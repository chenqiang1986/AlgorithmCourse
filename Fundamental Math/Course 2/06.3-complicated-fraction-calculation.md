# Lesson 6.3: Complicated Fraction Calculations
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

Fractions can contain several operations or even another fraction in the numerator or
denominator. In this lesson, work by **equivalent transformations** only. Keep the original
numbers visible and do not calculate a final numerical answer.

## 1. Read the Structure Before Changing It

Parentheses and fraction bars group parts of an expression. For example,

$$\frac{\frac{7}{9}+\frac{2}{9}}{\frac{5}{6}-\frac{1}{6}}$$

means “the top expression divided by the bottom expression.” The two additions/subtractions
inside are separate from the large division shown by the main fraction bar.

## 2. Rewrite Every Subtraction as Addition of a Negative Number

The expression above can be written with addition signs only:

$$\frac{\frac{7}{9}+\frac{2}{9}}{\frac{5}{6}+\left(-\frac{1}{6}\right)}$$

Nothing has been calculated. The fraction bar and all original number magnitudes remain.

## 3. Division by a Fraction Becomes Multiplication by Its Reciprocal

Dividing by a fraction can be rewritten as multiplying by the flipped fraction:

$$\frac{8}{3}\div\frac{5}{4}=\frac{8}{3}\times\frac{4}{5}$$

This is a transformation, not an invitation to multiply across. The four original numbers,
$8$, $3$, $5$, and $4$, are still present.

## 4. Same-Denominator Sums Can Become One Fraction

When fractions already have the same denominator, keep that denominator and combine the
numerators without evaluating their sum:

$$\frac{7}{12}+\frac{5}{12}+\left(-\frac{3}{12}\right)=\frac{7+5+(-3)}{12}$$

This form is useful because it separates the “numerator addition” from the denominator.

## 5. Rules for This Class

1. Transform only; do not find a final numerical value.
2. Use no new number magnitudes. A number may be given a negative sign when rewriting a
   subtraction as addition of a negative.
3. Preserve the grouping indicated by parentheses and fraction bars.
4. State the requested equivalent form exactly: for example, “only addition,” “multiply by a
   reciprocal,” or “one fraction.”

## 6. Reading Example: A Complex Fraction With Only Addition

**Example (Only Addition in a Complex Fraction).** Rewrite
$\dfrac{\frac{4}{11}-\frac{3}{11}}{\frac{8}{9}-\frac{2}{9}}$ so it contains no subtraction
signs. Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

**Solution:**

Replace each subtraction with addition of a negative fraction:

$$\frac{\frac{4}{11}+\left(-\frac{3}{11}\right)}{\frac{8}{9}+\left(-\frac{2}{9}\right)}$$

$\square$

## 8. Class Practice 2: One Fraction, Not a Final Answer

**Problem (One Fraction).** Rewrite $\dfrac{9}{14}+\dfrac{2}{14}-\dfrac{5}{14}$ as one
fraction with no subtraction signs. Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

<details>
<summary>One equivalent form</summary>

$$\frac{9+2+(-5)}{14}$$

</details>

## 9. Class Practice 3: Preserve a Complex Fraction

**Problem (Only Addition).** Rewrite
$\dfrac{\frac{6}{13}-\frac{4}{13}}{\frac{9}{10}-\frac{3}{10}}$ with no subtraction signs.
Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

<details>
<summary>One equivalent form</summary>

$$\frac{\frac{6}{13}+\left(-\frac{4}{13}\right)}{\frac{9}{10}+\left(-\frac{3}{10}\right)}$$

</details>

## 10. Class Practice 4: Two Transformations

**Problem (Reciprocal, Then Only Addition).** Rewrite
$\left(\dfrac{5}{6}-\dfrac{1}{6}\right)\div\dfrac{7}{9}$ as multiplication with no
subtraction signs. Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

<details>
<summary>One equivalent form</summary>

$$\left(\frac{5}{6}+\left(-\frac{1}{6}\right)\right)\times\frac{9}{7}$$

</details>

## Key Takeaways

- Fraction bars and parentheses identify the groups that must stay together.
- Subtraction can become addition of a negative fraction.
- Dividing by a fraction can become multiplying by its reciprocal.
- A correct equivalent form need not be evaluated.
