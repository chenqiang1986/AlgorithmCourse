# Lesson 14: Ratios, Proportions, Tax, and Discount Rates
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

Tax and discount questions are percent questions. A percent is a ratio out of $100$, so
we can use a proportion to connect a percentage with a dollar amount. In this lesson,
we focus on one direct question at a time: find a tax or discount amount, find a rate,
or find an original price.

## 1. Percent Is a Ratio Out of 100

For tax, compare the tax amount with the original (pre-tax) price:

$$\frac{\text{tax amount}}{\text{original price}}=\frac{\text{tax rate}}{100}.$$

For a discount, compare the amount removed with the original price:

$$\frac{\text{discount amount}}{\text{original price}}=\frac{\text{discount rate}}{100}.$$

The structure is the same in both situations.

| Situation | Part | Whole | Rate ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tax | tax amount | original price | tax rate $/100$ |
| Discount | discount amount | original price | discount rate $/100$ |

> **The whole is the original price.** A tax or discount is a part of that original
> price. Labeling the part and whole before writing a proportion prevents many errors.

## 2. Solving a Proportion With Cross Products

Suppose

$$\frac{a}{b}=\frac{c}{d}.$$

The two fractions are equal, so their cross products are equal:

$$a\cdot d=b\cdot c.$$

Then solve the resulting equation.

**Example (Cross products).** Solve $\frac{x}{48}=\frac{7.5}{100}$.

Multiply diagonally:

$$100x=48(7.5).$$

Calculate the right side:

$$100x=360.$$

Divide both sides by $100$:

$$x=3.60.$$

> **Cross-product pattern:** Multiply the numerator on one side by the denominator on
> the other side. Do this for both diagonal pairs, set the products equal, and solve.

## 3. Given the Rate and Original Price: Find the Tax Amount

**Example (Tax amount).** A purchase has an original price of $\$48.00$. The tax rate
is $7.5\%$. Find the tax amount.

**Solution:**

Let $t$ be the tax amount. Tax is $7.5$ out of every $100$ dollars of the original
price, so

$$\frac{t}{48}=\frac{7.5}{100}.$$

Cross-multiply:

$$100t=48(7.5)$$

$$100t=360$$

$$t=3.60.$$

The tax amount is **$\$3.60$**.

$\square$

## 4. Given the Tax Amount and Original Price: Find the Rate

**Example (Tax rate).** A $\$60.00$ purchase has a tax amount of $\$4.20$. What is the
tax rate?

**Solution:**

Let $r$ be the tax rate as a percent. Write tax over original price:

$$\frac{4.20}{60}=\frac{r}{100}.$$

Cross-multiply:

$$60r=4.20(100)$$

$$60r=420$$

$$r=7.$$

The tax rate is **$7\%$**.

$\square$

## 5. Given the Tax Rate and Tax Amount: Find the Original Price

**Example (Original price from tax).** A tax amount is $\$6.30$ at a tax rate of
$5\%$. What is the original price?

**Solution:**

Let $p$ be the original price. The proportion is

$$\frac{6.30}{p}=\frac{5}{100}.$$

Cross-multiply:

$$5p=6.30(100)$$

$$5p=630$$

$$p=126.$$

The original price is **$\$126.00$**.

$\square$

## 6. The Same Three Questions for a Discount

The equation changes only in the label. A discount amount is still a part of the
original price.

| Given | Find | Proportion setup |
| --- | --- | --- |
| discount rate and original price | discount amount $d$ | $\frac{d}{\text{original price}}=\frac{\text{rate}}{100}$ |
| discount amount and original price | discount rate $r$ | $\frac{\text{discount amount}}{\text{original price}}=\frac{r}{100}$ |
| discount rate and discount amount | original price $p$ | $\frac{\text{discount amount}}{p}=\frac{\text{rate}}{100}$ |

**Example (Discount amount).** A $\$84.00$ item has a $15\%$ discount. Find the
discount amount.

Let $d$ be the discount amount.

$$\frac{d}{84}=\frac{15}{100}$$

$$100d=84(15)$$

$$100d=1260$$

$$d=12.60.$$

The discount amount is **$\$12.60$**.

$\square$

**Problem.** A store charges $\$5.40$ tax on an item with an original price of

## 8. Common Mistakes

- **Using the wrong whole.** Put the original price under the tax or discount amount.
- **Putting the rate over $1$.** A percent rate belongs over $100$, such as
  $\frac{7}{100}$ for $7\%$.
- **Multiplying straight across.** In a proportion, multiply diagonally to make the
  cross-product equation.
- **Stopping at the cross-product equation.** After cross-multiplying, isolate the
  variable by dividing.
- **Forgetting the percent sign.** A result such as $7$ for a rate means $7\%$, not
  $7$ dollars.

## 9. Key Takeaways

- Tax and discount amounts are parts of an original price.
- Set up a percent proportion as $\frac{\text{part}}{\text{original price}}=
  \frac{\text{rate}}{100}$.
- Solve proportions by setting cross products equal, then solving the equation.
- The same proportion structure works for tax and discount questions.
