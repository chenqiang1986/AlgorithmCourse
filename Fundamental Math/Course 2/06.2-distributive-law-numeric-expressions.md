# Lesson 6.2: The Distributive Law With Numbers Only
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

This lesson uses only numbers to prepare for the distributive property with letters in
Lesson 7. The task is to replace parentheses with an equivalent expression while preserving
the numbers already present. Do not calculate the final numerical value.

## 1. One Factor Reaches Every Addend

Multiplication distributes across addition: a number outside parentheses multiplies every
addend inside.

$$6(8+3)=6(8)+6(3)$$

The right-hand expression has the same value, but its parentheses have been opened. Notice
that $6$, $8$, and $3$ were preserved; no product was evaluated.

## 2. Distributing Across a Difference

First read subtraction as addition of a negative number. Then distribute to both addends:

$$4(9-5)=4\bigl(9+(-5)\bigr)=4(9)+4(-5)$$

The outside $4$ must reach both $9$ and $-5$. It cannot be applied to only one part.

## 3. Negative Factors

The same rule works when the outside factor is negative:

$$(-3)(7+2)=(-3)(7)+(-3)(2)$$

No sign rule calculation is needed here. The transformation simply makes two multiplications
visible.

## 4. Rules for This Class

For every practice problem:

1. Write an equivalent expression with the requested parentheses removed or inserted.
2. Use only numbers already present in the original expression. A subtraction may be written
   as addition of a negative number.
3. Do not multiply or add to find a final numerical answer.

## 5. Reading Example: Open Parentheses Without Calculating

**Example (Open Parentheses).** Rewrite $5(12-7)$ without the parentheses. Use no numbers
other than $5$, $12$, and $7$, and do not calculate.

**Solution:**

Treat the difference as $12+(-7)$, then let $5$ multiply each addend:

$$5(12-7)=5\bigl(12+(-7)\bigr)=5(12)+5(-7)$$

$\square$

## 6. Class Practice 1: Distribute Over Addition

**Problem (Open Parentheses).** Rewrite $7(4+9)$ without parentheses. Use no new numbers and
do not calculate.

$$7(4)+7(9)$$

</details>

## 7. Class Practice 2: Distribute Over a Difference

**Problem (Only Addition).** Rewrite $8(11-6)$ with no subtraction signs and no outer
parentheses. Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

<details>
<summary>One equivalent form</summary>

$$8(11)+8(-6)$$

</details>

## 8. Class Practice 3: Factor Into Parentheses

**Problem (Make a Product of a Sum).** Rewrite $3(10)+3(-4)$ as one product with parentheses.
Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

<details>
<summary>One equivalent form</summary>

$$3\bigl(10+(-4)\bigr)$$

</details>

## 9. Class Practice 4: Negative Outside Factor

**Problem (Open Parentheses).** Rewrite $(-2)(9-5)$ with no subtraction signs and no outer
parentheses. Use no new numbers and do not calculate.

<details>
<summary>One equivalent form</summary>

$$(-2)(9)+(-2)(-5)$$

</details>

## Key Takeaways

- An outside factor multiplies every addend inside parentheses.
- A difference can be treated as a sum with a negative addend before distributing.
- Equivalent forms can be written without evaluating the products.
