This homework practices the four-step method from

**Directions:** For every problem, show all four steps. Give units where they apply.

## Part A: Translate Number Statements

### Problem 1: More Than

**Problem (More than).** Four more than three times a number is $25$. What is the number?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the number. Then $3x+4=25$, so $3x=21$ and $x=7$.
Check: $3(7)+4=25$. The number is **7**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 2: Less Than

**Problem (Less than).** Five less than twice a number is $13$. What is the number?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the number. “Five less than twice” gives $2x-5=13$.
Thus $2x=18$ and $x=9$. Check: $2(9)-5=13$. The number is **9**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 3: Parentheses Matter

**Problem (Parentheses matter).** Twice the number that is $4$ less than $x$ is $18$.
Find $x$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

The number that is $4$ less than $x$ is $x-4$, so $2(x-4)=18$.
Divide by $2$: $x-4=9$, and $x=13$. Check: $2(13-4)=18$.
The value of $x$ is **13**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 4: A Fraction

**Problem (A fraction).** One third of a number, plus $6$, is $14$. Find the number.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the number. Then $\frac{x}{3}+6=14$, so $\frac{x}{3}=8$ and
$x=24$. Check: $\frac{24}{3}+6=14$. The number is **24**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part B: Totals and Rates

### Problem 5: Museum Tickets

**Problem (Museum tickets).** A group pays a $\$7$ booking fee and $\$5$ per museum ticket.
The total is $\$42$. How many tickets did they buy?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $t$ be the number of tickets. The equation, in dollars, is $7+5t=42$.
Then $5t=35$ and $t=7$. Check: $7+5(7)=42$. They bought **7 tickets**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 6: Notebooks

**Problem (Notebooks).** A store charges $\$3$ per notebook and $\$2$ for a gift bag.
The total is $\$20$. How many notebooks were bought?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $n$ be the number of notebooks. Then $3n+2=20$.
Thus $3n=18$ and $n=6$. Check: $6$ notebooks cost $18$, plus $2$ for
the bag makes $20$. They bought **6 notebooks**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 7: Water Tank

**Problem (Water tank).** A tank starts with $18$ liters of water and loses $2$
liters each hour. After how many hours will it contain $8$ liters?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $h$ be the number of hours. The amount left is $18-2h$ liters, so
$18-2h=8$. Subtract $18$: $-2h=-10$, hence $h=5$.
Check: $18-2(5)=8$ liters. The tank reaches $8$ liters after **5 hours**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 8: Bags of Candy

**Problem (Bags of candy).** Each bag holds $6$ candies. There are $4$ candies
outside the bags and $40$ candies in all. How many bags are there?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $b$ be the number of bags. Then $6b+4=40$, so $6b=36$ and $b=6$.
Check: six bags hold $36$ candies; adding $4$ gives $40$.
There are **6 bags**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 9: Weekly Savings

**Problem (Weekly savings).** Lee already has $\$9$ and saves $\$4$ each week.
After how many weeks will Lee have $\$41$?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $w$ be the number of weeks. Then $9+4w=41$, so $4w=32$ and $w=8$.
Check: $9+4(8)=41$. Lee will have $\$41$ after **8 weeks**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 10: Ribbon Pieces

**Problem (Ribbon pieces).** A ribbon is cut into three equal pieces, with a
$5$-cm piece left over. The original ribbon was $38$ cm long. How long is
each equal piece?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $p$ be the length of each equal piece in centimeters. Then $3p+5=38$,
so $3p=33$ and $p=11$. Check: $3(11)+5=38$ cm.
Each equal piece is **11 cm** long.

$\square$

</details>

## Part C: Related Quantities

### Problem 11: Two Consecutive Integers

**Problem (Two consecutive integers).** Two consecutive integers have a sum of
$49$. Find both integers.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the smaller integer. The other is $x+1$, so
$x+(x+1)=49$. Thus $2x+1=49$, $2x=48$, and $x=24$.
Check: $24+25=49$. The integers are **24 and 25**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 12: Three Consecutive Integers

**Problem (Three consecutive integers).** Three consecutive integers have a
sum of $66$. Find all three.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the first integer. Then $x+(x+1)+(x+2)=66$.
So $3x+3=66$, $3x=63$, and $x=21$. Check: $21+22+23=66$.
The integers are **21, 22, and 23**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 13: Consecutive Even Integers

**Problem (Consecutive even integers).** Two consecutive even integers have a
sum of $46$. Find them.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $x$ be the smaller even integer. The next even integer is $x+2$.
Then $x+(x+2)=46$, so $2x=44$ and $x=22$.
Check: $22$ and $24$ are consecutive even integers and add to $46$.
They are **22 and 24**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 14: Rectangle

**Problem (Rectangle).** A rectangle's length is $4$ cm greater than its
width. Its perimeter is $40$ cm. Find both dimensions.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $w$ be the width in centimeters. The length is $w+4$.
The perimeter equation is $2w+2(w+4)=40$. Thus $4w+8=40$,
$4w=32$, and $w=8$. The length is $12$ cm.
Check: $2(8)+2(12)=40$ cm. The dimensions are **8 cm by 12 cm**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 15: Triangle

**Problem (Triangle).** An isosceles triangle has two equal sides. Its base
is $3$ cm longer than each equal side, and its perimeter is $36$ cm.
Find the length of each side.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $s$ be the length of each equal side in centimeters. The base is $s+3$.
Then $s+s+(s+3)=36$, so $3s+3=36$ and $s=11$.
The base is $14$ cm. Check: $11+11+14=36$ cm.
The side lengths are **11 cm, 11 cm, and 14 cm**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part D: Compare and Explain

### Problem 16: Equal Rental Prices

**Problem (Equal rental prices).** Shop A charges $\$12$ plus $\$2$ per hour to rent
a scooter. Shop B charges $\$4$ plus $\$4$ per hour. After how many hours will the
prices be equal?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $h$ be hours. Set the prices equal: $12+2h=4+4h$.
Subtract $2h$ and then $4$: $8=2h$, so $h=4$.
Check: both shops charge $\$20$ at four hours. The prices are equal after
**4 hours**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 17: Game Scores

**Problem (Game scores).** Jules starts with $7$ points and earns $5$ points
per round. Aria starts with $19$ points and earns $2$ points per round.
After how many rounds will their scores be equal?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $r$ be the number of rounds. Their scores match when
$7+5r=19+2r$. Subtract $2r$ and $7$: $3r=12$, so $r=4$.
Check: Jules has $7+5(4)=27$ points and Aria has $19+2(4)=27$.
Their scores are equal after **4 rounds**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 18: Fix the Equation

**Problem (Fix the equation).** There are $3$ bags with the same number of
marbles in each bag, plus $2$ loose marbles. There are $29$ marbles in all.
A student writes $3(x+2)=29$, where $x$ is the number in each bag.
Explain the mistake, write the correct equation, and find $x$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

The $2$ loose marbles are added once, not once per bag. The correct equation
is $3x+2=29$. Then $3x=27$ and $x=9$.
Check: three bags of nine have $27$ marbles; two loose marbles make $29$.
There are **9 marbles per bag**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 19: Ages

**Problem (Ages).** Mira is $4$ years older than Ben. In $3$ years, the sum of
their ages will be $30$ years. How old are they now?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $b$ be Ben's age now in years. Mira's age now is $b+4$.
In three years they will be $b+3$ and $b+7$, so
$(b+3)+(b+7)=30$. Thus $2b+10=30$ and $b=10$.
Mira is $14$. Check: in three years they will be $13$ and $17$,
which add to $30$. Ben is **10 years old** and Mira is **14 years old**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 20: Two Ticket Prices

**Problem (Two ticket prices).** A theater sold $11$ tickets in all. Adult
tickets cost $\$8$ each and child tickets cost $\$5$ each. The total collected was
$\$70$. How many of each ticket were sold?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:**

Let $a$ be the number of adult tickets. Then $11-a$ is the number of child
tickets. The revenue equation is $8a+5(11-a)=70$.
Distribute and combine: $8a+55-5a=70$, so $3a=15$ and $a=5$.
There were $11-5=6$ child tickets.
Check: $5+6=11$ tickets and $5(8)+6(5)=70$ dollars.
The theater sold **5 adult tickets and 6 child tickets**.

$\square$

</details>
