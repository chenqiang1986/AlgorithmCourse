# Homework: Lesson 13 — Geometry Word Problems With Linear Equations
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

This homework accompanies [Lesson 13](./13-geometry-linear-equation-word-problems.md).
For every problem, define the variable, write the formula expression or equation, solve,
and check the result in the story. Give length answers in the stated linear units and
area answers in square units. Use the specified value of $\pi$.

## Part A: Perimeter, Circumference, and Area

### Problem 1: Square Patio

**Problem (Square patio).** A square patio has perimeter $68$ ft. Find its side length and area.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $s$ be the side length. $4s=68$, so $s=17$ ft. Its area is
$17^2=289\text{ ft}^2$. The patio has side length **$17$ ft** and area
**$289\text{ ft}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 2: Rectangle Frame

**Problem (Rectangle frame).** A rectangular frame is $7$ cm longer than it is wide. Its
perimeter is $50$ cm. Find its dimensions and area.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $w$ be the width. The length is $w+7$, so
$2w+2(w+7)=50$. Thus $4w=36$ and $w=9$ cm. The length is $16$ cm, and the area is
$9(16)=144\text{ cm}^2$. The frame is **$9$ cm by $16$ cm**, with area
**$144\text{ cm}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 3: Circular Tabletop

**Problem (Circular tabletop).** A circular tabletop has circumference $44$ cm. Use
$\pi=\frac{22}{7}$ to find its radius and area.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $r$ be the radius. Then $2\left(\frac{22}{7}\right)r=44$, so
$r=7$ cm. Its area is $\frac{22}{7}(7^2)=154\text{ cm}^2$. The radius is **$7$ cm**
and the area is **$154\text{ cm}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 4: Banner

**Problem (Banner).** A rectangular banner's length is three times its width. Its perimeter
is $64$ in. Find its dimensions and area.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $w$ be the width. The length is $3w$, so $2w+2(3w)=64$.
Therefore $8w=64$ and $w=8$ in. The length is $24$ in, and the area is
$8(24)=192\text{ in}^2$. The banner is **$8$ in by $24$ in**, with area
**$192\text{ in}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part B: Circle and Square Comparisons

### Problem 5: Same Radius and Side

**Problem (Circle and square).** A circle's radius is the same as a square's side length.
Using $\pi=3$, the circle's circumference is $4$ cm greater than the square's perimeter.
Find the square's side length and area.

> **Hint:** Let $s$ be the side length of the square in centimeters.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** The circle circumference is $6s$ and the square perimeter is $4s$.
Thus $6s=4s+4$, so $2s=4$ and $s=2$ cm. The area is $2^2=4\text{ cm}^2$.
The side length is **$2$ cm** and the area is **$4\text{ cm}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 6: Decimal Length

**Problem (Circle and square).** A circle's radius is the same as a square's side length.
Using $\pi\approx3.14$, the circle's circumference is $7$ cm greater than the square's
perimeter. Find the square's side length and area to the nearest hundredth.

> **Hint:** Let $s$ be the side length of the square in centimeters.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** $6.28s=4s+7$, so $2.28s=7$ and $s\approx3.07$ cm. Using the unrounded
value, the area is about $9.43\text{ cm}^2$. The side length is **$3.07$ cm** and the
area is **$9.43\text{ cm}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part C: Circle and Rectangle Relationships

### Problem 7: Combined Boundaries

**Problem (Fountain and garden).** A rectangular garden's width is the same as the radius
of a circular fountain, and its length is $5$ m longer than its width. Using $\pi=3$,
the fountain's circumference and garden's perimeter add to $50$ m. Find the fountain's
radius, the garden's dimensions, and the garden's area.

> **Hint:** Let $r$ be the radius of the circular fountain in meters.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** The circumference is $6r$; the garden perimeter is $2r+2(r+5)=4r+10$.
So $6r+4r+10=50$, giving $r=4$ m. The garden is $4$ m by $9$ m and has area
$36\text{ m}^2$. The radius is **$4$ m**; the garden is **$4$ m by $9$ m**, with area
**$36\text{ m}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 8: Perimeter Is Greater

**Problem (Pond and patio).** A rectangular patio has a width that is twice the radius of
a circular pond. Its length is $4$ m more than twice the pond's radius. Using $\pi=3$,
the patio's perimeter is $14$ m greater than the pond's circumference. Find the pond's
radius and the patio's area.

> **Hint:** Let $r$ be the radius of the circular pond in meters.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** The pond circumference is $6r$. The patio perimeter is
$2(2r)+2(2r+4)=8r+8$. Thus $8r+8=6r+14$, so $2r=6$ and $r=3$ m. The patio is
$6$ m by $10$ m, with area $60\text{ m}^2$. The radius is **$3$ m** and the patio's
area is **$60\text{ m}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 9: Twice Plus a Fixed Length

**Problem (Track and field).** A rectangular field's width is three times the radius of a
circular track. Its length is $3$ m more than four times the track's radius. Using
$\pi=3$, the field's perimeter is $12$ m more than twice the track's circumference.
Find the track's radius and the field's area.

> **Hint:** Let $r$ be the radius of the circular track in meters.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** The track circumference is $6r$ and the field perimeter is
$2(3r)+2(4r+3)=14r+6$. So $14r+6=2(6r)+12$, giving $2r=6$ and $r=3$ m.
The field is $9$ m by $15$ m, so its area is $135\text{ m}^2$. The radius is
**$3$ m** and the area is **$135\text{ m}^2$**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part D: Model and Check

### Problem 10: Check a Proposed Answer

**Problem (Track and field).** A rectangular field's width is three times the radius of a
circular track, and its length is $3$ m more than four times the track's radius. Using
$\pi=3$, the field's perimeter is $12$ m more than twice the track's circumference. A
student says the radius is $4$ m. Is the student correct? Solve the equation and explain.

> **Hint:** Let $r$ be the radius of the circular track in meters.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** The equation is $2(3r)+2(4r+3)=2(6r)+12$, or
$14r+6=12r+12$. Thus $2r=6$ and $r=3$ m. The student is **not correct**: if $r=4$,
the field perimeter would be $62$ m, while twice the circumference plus $12$ would be
$60$ m. The correct radius is **$3$ m**.

$\square$

</details>
