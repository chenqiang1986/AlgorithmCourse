# Homework: Lesson 6.1 — Generalized Addition and the Commutative Law
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

This homework practices the skills from
[Lesson 6.1](./06.1-generalized-addition-commutative-law.md). Rewrite each expression into
the requested equivalent form. These are **transformation** problems: do **not** calculate a
final numerical value. Keep every original number magnitude, and keep each negative sign
attached to its number.

## Part A: Rewrite Subtraction as Addition

For Problems 1–5, use addition signs only. Do not rearrange the addends.

### Problem 1

Rewrite: $8-13+6-4$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$8+(-13)+6+(-4)$$

</details>

### Problem 2

Rewrite: $-5+12-9+3$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-5+12+(-9)+3$$

</details>

### Problem 3

Rewrite: $14-7-2+11$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$14+(-7)+(-2)+11$$

</details>

### Problem 4

Rewrite: $3-10+5-8+1$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$3+(-10)+5+(-8)+1$$

</details>

### Problem 5

Rewrite: $-6-4+15-12$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-6+(-4)+15+(-12)$$

</details>

## Part B: Reorder Signed Addends

For Problems 6–10, rewrite the expression to satisfy the stated arrangement. You may use
ordinary addition and subtraction or addition of negative numbers. Do not calculate.

### Problem 6

Rewrite $7-5+2-9+4$ with all positive addends first in their original order, followed by
all negative addends in their original order.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$7+2+4-5-9$$

</details>

### Problem 7

Rewrite $11-3+3-11+6-6$ so that every number is next to its opposite.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$11-11-3+3+6-6$$

</details>

### Problem 8

Rewrite $4-12+7-2+9$ from greatest absolute value to least absolute value.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-12+9+7+4-2$$

</details>

### Problem 9

Rewrite $10-4+1-8+6$ from least absolute value to greatest absolute value.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$1-4+6-8+10$$

</details>

### Problem 10

Rewrite $13-2+5-7+1-10$ with all negative addends first, from least absolute value to
greatest absolute value; then write the positive addends in their original order.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-2-7-10+13+5+1$$

</details>

## Part C: Show the Three Transformations

For Problems 11–15:

1. rewrite using addition only;
2. rearrange as directed; and
3. rewrite using ordinary addition and subtraction only, with no written negative numbers.

Do not calculate.

### Problem 11

Start with $5-9+3-2$. In Step 2, put positive addends first in their original order.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$5-9+3-2=5+(-9)+3+(-2)$$

$$=5+3+(-9)+(-2)=5+3-9-2$$

</details>

### Problem 12

Start with $8-6+6-8+4$. In Step 2, place each opposite pair together, with the remaining
addend last.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$8-6+6-8+4=8+(-6)+6+(-8)+4$$

$$=8+(-8)+(-6)+6+4=8-8-6+6+4$$

</details>

### Problem 13

Start with $2-11+7-5+9$. In Step 2, arrange the signed addends from greatest absolute value
to least absolute value.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$2-11+7-5+9=2+(-11)+7+(-5)+9$$

$$=(-11)+9+7+(-5)+2=-11+9+7-5+2$$

</details>

### Problem 14

Start with $12-3+4-12+3-4$. In Step 2, group all opposite pairs.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$12-3+4-12+3-4=12+(-3)+4+(-12)+3+(-4)$$

$$=12+(-12)+(-3)+3+4+(-4)=12-12-3+3+4-4$$

</details>

### Problem 15

Start with $6-1+10-6+1-10$. In Step 2, group the opposite pairs in order of increasing
absolute value.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$6-1+10-6+1-10=6+(-1)+10+(-6)+1+(-10)$$

$$=(-1)+1+6+(-6)+10+(-10)=-1+1+6-6+10-10$$

</details>

## Reminder

Changing subtraction to addition of a negative and rearranging signed addends preserve an
expression's value. The goal here is a correctly structured equivalent expression, not its
final value.
