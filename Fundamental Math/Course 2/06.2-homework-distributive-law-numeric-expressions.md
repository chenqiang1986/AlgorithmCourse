# Homework: Lesson 6.2 — The Distributive Law With Numeric Expressions
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

This homework practices the skills from
[Lesson 6.2](./06.2-distributive-law-numeric-expressions.md). Rewrite each expression in the
requested equivalent form. Do **not** calculate products, sums, or a final numerical value.
Use $\times$ between numerical factors, and use only the number magnitudes in the original
expression.

## Part A: Open Parentheses

For Problems 1–5, remove the parentheses and simplify signs without calculating products.

### Problem 1

Rewrite: $4(7+5)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$4 \times 7+4 \times 5$$

</details>

### Problem 2

Rewrite: $6(11-3)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$6 \times 11-6 \times 3$$

</details>

### Problem 3

Rewrite: $(-2)(8+9)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-2 \times 8-2 \times 9$$

</details>

### Problem 4

Rewrite: $(-5)(4-7)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-5 \times 4+5 \times 7$$

</details>

### Problem 5

Rewrite: $9(-3+6)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-9 \times 3+9 \times 6$$

</details>

## Part B: Open Multiple Products

For Problems 6–10, remove all parentheses and simplify signs. Do not calculate products.

### Problem 6

Rewrite: $3(8-5)+2(7-4)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$3 \times 8-3 \times 5+2 \times 7-2 \times 4$$

</details>

### Problem 7

Rewrite: $5(2-9)-4(6-1)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$5 \times 2-5 \times 9-4 \times 6+4 \times 1$$

</details>

### Problem 8

Rewrite: $(-3)(4+7)+2(5-8)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-3 \times 4-3 \times 7+2 \times 5-2 \times 8$$

</details>

### Problem 9

Rewrite: $6(3-2)-(-4)(5-1)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$6 \times 3-6 \times 2+4 \times 5-4 \times 1$$

</details>

### Problem 10

Rewrite: $(-2)(9-4)-3(6-8)$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$-2 \times 9+2 \times 4-3 \times 6+3 \times 8$$

</details>


## Reminder

The distributive law sends the outside factor to **every** addend inside parentheses. Whether
opening or factoring, preserve the expression's numbers and signs; evaluating products is not
part of this homework.
