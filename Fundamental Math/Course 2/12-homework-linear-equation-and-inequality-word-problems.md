# Homework: Lesson 12 — Linear Equation and Inequality Word Problems
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

This homework accompanies [Lesson 12](./12-linear-equation-and-inequality-word-problems.md).
For each problem, define a variable with units, write an equation or inequality, solve, and
check in context. Counts must be nonnegative whole numbers.

## Part A: Exact Totals

### Problem 1: Admission

**Problem (Admission).** A museum charges a $\$9$ entry fee and $\$4$ for each audio guide.
The bill is $\$29$. How many guides were rented?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $g$ be guides. $9+4g=29$, so $g=5$. Check: $9+4(5)=29$.
**5 guides** were rented.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 2: Savings

**Problem (Savings).** Nia has $\$17$ and saves $\$6$ each week. After how many weeks will
she have $\$59$?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $w$ be weeks. $17+6w=59$, so $6w=42$ and $w=7$.
It takes **7 weeks**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 3: Temperature

**Problem (Temperature).** It is $4$ degrees Celsius at noon and rises $3$ degrees per hour.
After how many hours will it be $25$ degrees Celsius?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $h$ be hours. $4+3h=25$, so $h=7$. Check: $4+3(7)=25$.
It takes **7 hours**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 4: Perimeter

**Problem (Garden).** A rectangular garden is $5$ m longer than its width and has perimeter
$38$ m. Find its dimensions.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $w$ be width in meters; length is $w+5$. Then
$2w+2(w+5)=38$, so $4w=28$ and $w=7$. The length is $12$ m.
The dimensions are **$7$ m by $12$ m**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 5: Consecutive Integers

**Problem (Consecutive integers).** Three consecutive integers have a sum of $96$. Find them.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $x$ be the first integer. $x+(x+1)+(x+2)=96$, so $3x=93$ and
$x=31$. The integers are **31, 32, and 33**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 6: Equal Costs

**Problem (Equipment rental).** Store A charges $\$15$ plus $\$2$ per hour. Store B charges
$\$3$ plus $\$4$ per hour. When do they cost the same?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $h$ be hours. $15+2h=3+4h$, so $12=2h$ and $h=6$.
Both cost $\$27$ at **6 hours**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part B: Limits and Inequalities

### Problem 7: Maximum Cost

**Problem (Markers).** A teacher has at most $\$45$ for marker packs costing $\$7$ each.
What is the greatest number of packs the teacher can buy?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $p$ be packs. $7p\leq45$, so $p\leq6\tfrac{3}{7}$. Since packs are
whole, the maximum is **6 packs**. Six cost $\$42$; seven cost $\$49$.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 8: Minimum Score

**Problem (Quiz points).** Jin has $38$ points and earns $6$ points per remaining quiz. He
needs at least $68$ points. How many quizzes must he complete?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $q$ be quizzes. $38+6q\geq68$, so $6q\geq30$ and $q\geq5$.
He needs **at least 5 quizzes**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 9: Capacity

**Problem (Elevator).** An elevator holds no more than $900$ kg. A $240$-kg crate is inside,
and each added box weighs $55$ kg. How many boxes can be added?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $b$ be boxes. $240+55b\leq900$, so $55b\leq660$ and $b\leq12$.
At most **12 boxes** can be added.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 10: Fundraiser

**Problem (Fundraiser).** A team has $\$125$ and earns $\$18$ per car washed. It wants more
than $\$250$. How many cars must it wash?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $c$ be cars. $125+18c>250$, so $c>6\tfrac{17}{18}$. The smallest
whole number is **7 cars**. Seven yields $\$251$.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 11: Time Limit

**Problem (Walk).** A hiker has walked $2.5$ miles and walks $0.8$ mile per hour. She may
walk at most $6.5$ miles total. For how many more hours can she walk?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $h$ be hours. $2.5+0.8h\leq6.5$, so $0.8h\leq4$ and $h\leq5$.
She can walk for **at most 5 more hours**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 12: Less Expensive Plan

**Problem (Printing plans).** Plan A costs $\$6$ plus $\$0.20$ per page. Plan B costs
$\$14$ plus $\$0.10$ per page. For how many pages is Plan A less expensive?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $p$ be pages. $6+0.20p<14+0.10p$, so $0.10p<8$ and $p<80$.
For whole pages, Plan A is cheaper for **$0$ through $79$ pages**.

$\square$

</details>

## Part C: Heads and Legs

### Problem 13: Chickens and Bunnies

**Problem (Farm pen).** A pen has $27$ chickens and bunnies and $74$ legs. How many of each
animal are there?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $b$ be bunnies, so chickens are $27-b$. Then
$4b+2(27-b)=74$. Thus $2b=20$, so $b=10$ and there are $17$ chickens.
The pen has **10 bunnies and 17 chickens**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 14: Dogs and Birds

**Problem (Pet shelter).** A shelter has $34$ dogs and birds in all and $104$ legs. How many
dogs and birds are there?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $d$ be dogs; birds are $34-d$. $4d+2(34-d)=104$, so
$2d=36$ and $d=18$. There are $16$ birds: **18 dogs and 16 birds**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 15: Tricycles and Bicycles

**Problem (Toy shop).** A display has $40$ bicycles and tricycles and $98$ wheels. How many
tricycles are displayed?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $t$ be tricycles; bicycles are $40-t$. Then
$3t+2(40-t)=98$, so $t=18$. There are **18 tricycles** and $22$ bicycles.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 16: Harder Heads and Legs

**Problem (Insects and spiders).** A class observes $36$ insects and spiders. Insects have
$6$ legs, spiders have $8$ legs, and there are $250$ legs. How many spiders are there?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $s$ be spiders; insects are $36-s$. $8s+6(36-s)=250$,
so $2s=34$ and $s=17$. The class observed **17 spiders** and $19$ insects.

$\square$

</details>

## Part D: Choose and Explain

### Problem 17: Which Symbol?

**Problem (Library books).** Sam may borrow no more than $8$ books. Let $b$ be the number
borrowed. Write an inequality and all possible whole-number values.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** “No more than” means $b\leq8$. The possible values are
**$0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7,$ and $8$**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 18: Fix the Model

**Problem (Bracelets).** A student has $\$11$ and earns $\$4$ per bracelet. She needs at
least $\$35$ but writes $11+4b=35$. Correct the model and find the least number sold.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** “At least” requires $11+4b\geq35$, not equality. Thus $4b\geq24$,
so $b\geq6$. She must sell **at least 6 bracelets**.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 19: Boundary Check

**Problem (Game rental).** A game store charges $\$5$ plus $\$3$ per day. Ava has $\$25$.
For how many days can she rent the game? Check the largest answer.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $d$ be days. $5+3d\leq25$, so $d\leq6\tfrac{2}{3}$.
The maximum is **6 days**: six cost $\$23$, but seven cost $\$26$.

$\square$

</details>

### Problem 20: Compare Delivery Services

**Problem (Delivery services).** Service X charges $\$10$ plus $\$1.50$ per mile. Service Y
charges $\$22$ plus $\$0.75$ per mile. For what distances is X no more expensive than Y?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

**Solution:** Let $m$ be miles. $10+1.50m\leq22+0.75m$, so $0.75m\leq12$ and
$m\leq16$. Service X is no more expensive for **$16$ miles or fewer**.

$\square$

</details>
