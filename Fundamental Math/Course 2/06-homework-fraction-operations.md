# Homework: Lessons 5–6 — Fraction Operations
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

This homework covers
[05-fraction-multiplication-division.md](./05-fraction-multiplication-division.md)
(fraction signs, multiplication, and division) and
[06-fraction-addition.md](./06-fraction-addition.md)

## Part A: Fraction Signs, Multiplication, and Division

### Problem 1

Determine the sign of $\dfrac{5}{-12}$ and rewrite it in standard form.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Numerator ($5$) and denominator ($-12$) have different signs, so the fraction is negative.
Move the minus sign to the front:

$$\frac{5}{-12} = -\frac{5}{12}$$

The answer is **$-\dfrac{5}{12}$, negative**.

</details>

### Problem 2

Evaluate: $\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right) \times \dfrac{9}{10}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Different signs, so the product is negative. Multiply the absolute values:

$$\left(-\frac{2}{3}\right) \times \frac{9}{10} = -\left(\frac{2 \times 9}{3 \times 10}\right) = -\frac{18}{30}$$

$18$ and $30$ share the factor $6$, so reduce:

$$-\frac{18}{30} = -\frac{3 \times 6}{5 \times 6} = -\frac{3}{5}$$

The answer is **$-\dfrac{3}{5}$**.

</details>

### Problem 3

Evaluate: $\dfrac{5}{6} \times \left(-\dfrac{4}{15}\right)$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Different signs, so the product is negative:

$$\frac{5}{6} \times \left(-\frac{4}{15}\right) = -\left(\frac{5 \times 4}{6 \times 15}\right) = -\frac{20}{90}$$

$20$ and $90$ share the factor $10$, so reduce:

$$-\frac{20}{90} = -\frac{2 \times 10}{9 \times 10} = -\frac{2}{9}$$

The answer is **$-\dfrac{2}{9}$**.

</details>

### Problem 4

Evaluate: $\dfrac{7}{8} \div \dfrac{14}{5}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Flip the divisor to its reciprocal and multiply:

$$\frac{7}{8} \div \frac{14}{5} = \frac{7}{8} \times \frac{5}{14} = \frac{7 \times 5}{8 \times 14} = \frac{35}{112}$$

$35$ and $112$ share the factor $7$, so reduce:

$$\frac{35}{112} = \frac{5 \times 7}{16 \times 7} = \frac{5}{16}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{5}{16}$**.

</details>

### Problem 5

Evaluate: $\left(-\dfrac{9}{10}\right) \div \left(-\dfrac{3}{5}\right)$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Flip the divisor to its reciprocal and multiply:

$$\left(-\frac{9}{10}\right) \div \left(-\frac{3}{5}\right) = \left(-\frac{9}{10}\right) \times \left(-\frac{5}{3}\right)$$

Same sign (both negative), so the product is positive:

$$\left(-\frac{9}{10}\right) \times \left(-\frac{5}{3}\right) = \frac{9 \times 5}{10 \times 3} = \frac{45}{30}$$

$45$ and $30$ share the factor $15$, so reduce:

$$\frac{45}{30} = \frac{3 \times 15}{2 \times 15} = \frac{3}{2}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{3}{2}$**.

</details>

### Problem 6

Evaluate: $\dfrac{2}{3} \times \left(-\dfrac{3}{4}\right) \times \dfrac{5}{6}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Count the negative factors: only $-\dfrac34$ is negative — one negative factor, which is
odd, so the product is negative. Multiply the absolute values:

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{5}{6} = \frac{2 \times 3 \times 5}{3 \times 4 \times 6} = \frac{30}{72}$$

$30$ and $72$ share the factor $6$, so reduce:

$$\frac{30}{72} = \frac{5 \times 6}{12 \times 6} = \frac{5}{12}$$

Applying the negative sign, the answer is **$-\dfrac{5}{12}$**.

</details>

### Problem 7

A recipe calls for $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of sugar for one full batch. How much sugar is needed
for $\dfrac{2}{3}$ of a batch? Write an expression and evaluate it.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\dfrac23$ of $\dfrac34$ cup means multiplying:

$$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 3} = \frac{6}{12}$$

$6$ and $12$ share the factor $6$, so reduce:

$$\frac{6}{12} = \frac{1 \times 6}{2 \times 6} = \frac{1}{2}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{1}{2}$ cup**.

</details>

## Part B: Adding Fractions and Reducing to Simplest Form

### Problem 8

Evaluate: $\dfrac{5}{12} + \dfrac{7}{12}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The denominators already match, so add the numerators and keep the denominator:

$$\frac{5}{12} + \frac{7}{12} = \frac{5+7}{12} = \frac{12}{12}$$

$12$ and $12$ share the factor $12$, so reduce:

$$\frac{12}{12} = \frac{1 \times 12}{1 \times 12} = 1$$

The answer is **$1$**.

</details>

### Problem 9

Evaluate: $-\dfrac{7}{10} + \dfrac{3}{10}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The denominators already match, so add the numerators using the integer addition rule:

$$-\frac{7}{10} + \frac{3}{10} = \frac{-7+3}{10} = \frac{-4}{10} = -\frac{4}{10}$$

$4$ and $10$ share the factor $2$, so reduce:

$$-\frac{4}{10} = -\frac{2 \times 2}{5 \times 2} = -\frac{2}{5}$$

The answer is **$-\dfrac{2}{5}$**.

</details>

### Problem 10

Evaluate: $\dfrac{3}{8} + \dfrac{1}{6}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply each fraction so both denominators become $8 \times 6 = 48$:

$$\frac{3}{8} \times \frac{6}{6} = \frac{18}{48} \qquad \qquad \frac{1}{6} \times \frac{8}{8} = \frac{8}{48}$$

Add the numerators:

$$\frac{18}{48} + \frac{8}{48} = \frac{18+8}{48} = \frac{26}{48}$$

$26$ and $48$ share the factor $2$, so reduce:

$$\frac{26}{48} = \frac{13 \times 2}{24 \times 2} = \frac{13}{24}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{13}{24}$**.

</details>

### Problem 11

Evaluate: $-\dfrac{5}{6} + \dfrac{7}{9}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply each fraction so both denominators become $6 \times 9 = 54$:

$$-\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{9}{9} = -\frac{45}{54} \qquad \qquad \frac{7}{9} \times \frac{6}{6} = \frac{42}{54}$$

Add the numerators:

$$-\frac{45}{54} + \frac{42}{54} = \frac{-45+42}{54} = \frac{-3}{54} = -\frac{3}{54}$$

$3$ and $54$ share the factor $3$, so reduce:

$$-\frac{3}{54} = -\frac{1 \times 3}{18 \times 3} = -\frac{1}{18}$$

The answer is **$-\dfrac{1}{18}$**.

</details>

### Problem 12

Reduce $\dfrac{18}{24}$ to simplest form.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$18$ and $24$ share the factor $c = 6$: $18 = 3 \times 6$ and $24 = 4 \times 6$, so:

$$\frac{18}{24} = \frac{3 \times 6}{4 \times 6} = \frac{3}{4}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{3}{4}$**.

</details>

### Problem 13

Evaluate: $\dfrac{5}{6} + \dfrac{1}{4}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply each fraction so both denominators become $6 \times 4 = 24$:

$$\frac{5}{6} \times \frac{4}{4} = \frac{20}{24} \qquad \qquad \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{6}{6} = \frac{6}{24}$$

Add the numerators:

$$\frac{20}{24} + \frac{6}{24} = \frac{20+6}{24} = \frac{26}{24}$$

$26$ and $24$ share the factor $2$, so reduce:

$$\frac{26}{24} = \frac{13 \times 2}{12 \times 2} = \frac{13}{12}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{13}{12}$**.

</details>

### Problem 14

On Monday a runner ran $\dfrac{2}{5}$ mile, and on Tuesday ran $\dfrac{3}{4}$ mile. Write an
expression for the total distance run over the two days, and evaluate it.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The total distance is the sum $\dfrac25 + \dfrac34$. Multiply each fraction so both
denominators become $5 \times 4 = 20$:

$$\frac{2}{5} \times \frac{4}{4} = \frac{8}{20} \qquad \qquad \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{5}{5} = \frac{15}{20}$$

Add the numerators:

$$\frac{8}{20} + \frac{15}{20} = \frac{8+15}{20} = \frac{23}{20}$$

$23$ is prime and doesn't divide $20$, so $\dfrac{23}{20}$ is already in simplest form.

The answer is **$\dfrac{23}{20}$ miles**.

</details>

## Part C: Composite Expressions — Multiplication and Addition Together

These expressions mix multiplication/division with addition. Do the multiplication and
division first, left to right, then the addition — the same order-of-operations rule used
for integers, applied with the fraction rules from Lessons 5–6.

### Problem 15

Evaluate: $\dfrac{1}{2} \times \dfrac{2}{3} + \dfrac{1}{4}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply first:

$$\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1 \times 2}{2 \times 3} = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$$

Now add, using a common denominator of $3 \times 4 = 12$:

$$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{4}{4} = \frac{4}{12} \qquad \qquad \frac{1}{4} \times \frac{3}{3} = \frac{3}{12}$$

$$\frac{4}{12} + \frac{3}{12} = \frac{4+3}{12} = \frac{7}{12}$$

$7$ is prime and doesn't divide $12$, so $\dfrac{7}{12}$ is already in simplest form.

The answer is **$\dfrac{7}{12}$**.

</details>

### Problem 16

Evaluate: $-\dfrac{3}{5} + \dfrac{2}{3} \times \dfrac{9}{10}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply first:

$$\frac{2}{3} \times \frac{9}{10} = \frac{2 \times 9}{3 \times 10} = \frac{18}{30}$$

$18$ and $30$ share the factor $6$, so reduce:

$$\frac{18}{30} = \frac{3 \times 6}{5 \times 6} = \frac{3}{5}$$

Now add:

$$-\frac{3}{5} + \frac{3}{5} = \frac{-3+3}{5} = \frac{0}{5} = 0$$

The answer is **$0$**.

</details>

### Problem 17

Evaluate: $\dfrac{5}{6} \times \left(-\dfrac{2}{5}\right) + \dfrac{7}{9}$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply first. Different signs, so the product is negative:

$$\frac{5}{6} \times \left(-\frac{2}{5}\right) = -\left(\frac{5 \times 2}{6 \times 5}\right) = -\frac{10}{30}$$

$10$ and $30$ share the factor $10$, so reduce:

$$-\frac{10}{30} = -\frac{1 \times 10}{3 \times 10} = -\frac{1}{3}$$

Now add. Since $9 = 3 \times 3$, denominator $9$ already works as a common denominator:

$$-\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{3}{3} = -\frac{3}{9}$$

$$-\frac{3}{9} + \frac{7}{9} = \frac{-3+7}{9} = \frac{4}{9}$$

$4$ and $9$ share no common factor greater than $1$, so $\dfrac{4}{9}$ is already in
simplest form.

The answer is **$\dfrac{4}{9}$**.

</details>

### Problem 18

Evaluate: $\dfrac{3}{4} + \dfrac{2}{7} \times \left(-\dfrac{7}{8}\right)$

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply first. Different signs, so the product is negative:

$$\frac{2}{7} \times \left(-\frac{7}{8}\right) = -\left(\frac{2 \times 7}{7 \times 8}\right) = -\frac{14}{56}$$

$14$ and $56$ share the factor $14$, so reduce:

$$-\frac{14}{56} = -\frac{1 \times 14}{4 \times 14} = -\frac{1}{4}$$

Now add — the denominators already match:

$$\frac{3}{4} + \left(-\frac{1}{4}\right) = \frac{3 + (-1)}{4} = \frac{2}{4}$$

$2$ and $4$ share the factor $2$, so reduce:

$$\frac{2}{4} = \frac{1 \times 2}{2 \times 2} = \frac{1}{2}$$

The answer is **$\dfrac{1}{2}$**.

</details>

### Problem 19

A bucket already has $\dfrac{1}{3}$ gallon of blue paint in it. A painter adds $\dfrac{3}{4}$
of a $\dfrac{2}{5}$-gallon can of white paint. Write an expression for the total amount of
paint in the bucket, and evaluate it.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\dfrac34$ of $\dfrac25$ gallon means multiplying, and that amount is added to the paint
already in the bucket:

$$\frac{1}{3} + \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5}$$

Multiply first:

$$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{2}{5} = \frac{3 \times 2}{4 \times 5} = \frac{6}{20}$$

$6$ and $20$ share the factor $2$, so reduce:

$$\frac{6}{20} = \frac{3 \times 2}{10 \times 2} = \frac{3}{10}$$

Now add, using a common denominator of $3 \times 10 = 30$:

$$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{10}{10} = \frac{10}{30} \qquad \qquad \frac{3}{10} \times \frac{3}{3} = \frac{9}{30}$$

$$\frac{10}{30} + \frac{9}{30} = \frac{10+9}{30} = \frac{19}{30}$$

$19$ is prime and doesn't divide $30$, so $\dfrac{19}{30}$ is already in simplest form.

The answer is **$\dfrac{19}{30}$ gallon**.

</details>

### Problem 20

A baker uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ cup of flour for the bread's base, $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of $\dfrac{2}{3}$
cup of flour for the topping, and $\dfrac{1}{5}$ cup of flour for dusting the pan. Write an
expression for the total flour used, and evaluate it.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\dfrac13$ of $\dfrac23$ cup means multiplying; all three amounts are added together:

$$\frac{3}{4} + \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{5}$$

Multiply first:

$$\frac{1}{3} \times \frac{2}{3} = \frac{1 \times 2}{3 \times 3} = \frac{2}{9}$$

Add the first two terms, using a common denominator of $4 \times 9 = 36$:

$$\frac{3}{4} \times \frac{9}{9} = \frac{27}{36} \qquad \qquad \frac{2}{9} \times \frac{4}{4} = \frac{8}{36}$$

$$\frac{27}{36} + \frac{8}{36} = \frac{27+8}{36} = \frac{35}{36}$$

Add the last term, using a common denominator of $36 \times 5 = 180$:

$$\frac{35}{36} \times \frac{5}{5} = \frac{175}{180} \qquad \qquad \frac{1}{5} \times \frac{36}{36} = \frac{36}{180}$$

$$\frac{175}{180} + \frac{36}{180} = \frac{175+36}{180} = \frac{211}{180}$$

$211$ is prime and doesn't divide $180$, so $\dfrac{211}{180}$ is already in simplest form.

The answer is **$\dfrac{211}{180}$ cup**.

</details>
