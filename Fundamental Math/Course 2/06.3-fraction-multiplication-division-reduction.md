# Lesson 6.3: Complicated Fractions — Multiplication, Division, and Reduction
*Fundamental Math / Course 2*

[Lesson 5](./05-fraction-multiplication-division.md) introduced fraction multiplication and
division. This lesson handles longer chains such as $\frac{5}{8}\times\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{8}{11}$
and fractions stacked on fractions, and shows how to **reduce before multiplying** so the
numbers stay small. [Lesson 6.4](./06.4-fraction-addition-different-denominators.md) covers addition.

**Rules for this class:** work by equivalent transformations. Leave products in factor form
(write $3\times5$, not $15$) and use only the numbers already in the problem.

## 1. Multiplying Fractions

Multiply the numerators together and the denominators together:

$$\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a\times c}{b\times d}$$

The same works for three or more fractions: one big numerator, one big denominator.

$$\frac{2}{3}\times\frac{5}{7}\times\frac{4}{11}=\frac{2\times5\times4}{3\times7\times11}$$

## 2. Signs

Decide the sign first, then multiply the absolute values. An **even** number of negative
factors gives $+$; an **odd** number gives $-$.

$$\left(-\frac{4}{9}\right)\times\frac{5}{13}=-\frac{4\times5}{9\times13}
\qquad
\left(-\frac{4}{9}\right)\times\left(-\frac{5}{13}\right)=\frac{4\times5}{9\times13}$$

## 3. Division Is Multiplication by the Reciprocal

The **reciprocal** of $\dfrac{c}{d}$ is $\dfrac{d}{c}$. Dividing by a fraction means
multiplying by its reciprocal:

$$\frac{a}{b}\div\frac{c}{d}=\frac{a}{b}\times\frac{d}{c}=\frac{a\times d}{b\times c}$$

Only the fraction **after** the $\div$ is flipped. The sign stays where it is.

**A fraction over a fraction is also a division.** The long bar means "top $\div$ bottom":

$$\dfrac{\quad\dfrac{3}{5}\quad}{\quad\dfrac{3}{7}\quad}=\frac{3}{5}\div\frac{3}{7}=\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{7}{3}$$

## 4. Reduction: Cancel Common Factors

If a factor appears in a numerator **and** in a denominator, they cancel (this is
$\frac{a\times c}{b\times c}=\frac{a}{b}$ from [Lesson 6](./06-fraction-addition.md), read in
reverse). Cancel **before** multiplying — any numerator may cancel with any denominator,
even across different fractions.

Here is the full reasoning, one step at a time:

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{6}{7}\times\frac{7}{11}&=\frac{6\times7}{7\times11}&&\text{multiply across}\\
&=\frac{6\times7}{11\times7}&&\text{reorder the bottom factors}\\
&=\frac{6}{11}\times\frac{7}{7}&&\text{split into two fractions}\\
&=\frac{6}{11}\times1&&\frac{7}{7}=1\\
&=\frac{6}{11}&&
\end{aligned}$$

Cancelling is just this: a factor over itself is $1$, and multiplying by $1$ changes nothing.
In practice we skip the middle steps and cross out the matching pair.

**One more example (cross-cancellation).** The $5$ is in the top of the first fraction and
the bottom of the second:

$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{7}{5}&=\frac{5\times7}{6\times5}\\
&=\frac{7\times5}{6\times5}&&\text{reorder the top factors}\\
&=\frac{7}{6}\times\frac{5}{5}\\
&=\frac{7}{6}\times1=\frac{7}{6}
\end{aligned}$$

Each factor cancels with exactly **one** partner, and only a *numerator* can cancel with a
*denominator*. Two numerators never cancel, and a top number never cancels with a
number in the same fraction's top.

Cancelling a number against itself leaves $1$, not $0$: $\dfrac{7\times 5}{7\times 3}=\dfrac{5}{3}$.

## Reading Example 1: Reduce Across Three Fractions

**Example.** Simplify $\dfrac{5}{8}\times\dfrac{3}{5}\times\dfrac{8}{11}$.

**Solution:**

$$\frac{5}{8}\times\frac{3}{5}\times\frac{8}{11}=\frac{5\times3\times8}{8\times5\times11}$$

The $5$ in the top cancels the $5$ in the bottom, and the $8$ cancels the $8$:

$$=\frac{3}{11}$$

$\square$

## Reading Example 2: Division, Signs, and Reduction Together

**Example.** Simplify $\dfrac{2}{9}\times\left(-\dfrac{9}{13}\right)\div\dfrac{2}{5}$.

**Solution:**

*Step 1 — flip the divisor:*

$$\frac{2}{9}\times\left(-\frac{9}{13}\right)\times\frac{5}{2}$$

*Step 2 — one negative factor, so the result is negative:*

$$-\frac{2\times9\times5}{9\times13\times2}$$

*Step 3 — cancel the $2$ and the $9$:*

$$-\frac{5}{13}$$

$\square$

## Common Mistakes

### Flipping the wrong fraction

$\dfrac{3}{4}\div\dfrac{5}{7}$ becomes $\dfrac{3}{4}\times\dfrac{7}{5}$, not $\dfrac{4}{3}\times\dfrac{5}{7}$.

### Cancelling across an addition

Cancelling is only for **products**. In $\dfrac{3+7}{7}$ the $7$s cannot cancel, because
$7$ in the top is added, not multiplied. (Addition is the topic of Lesson 6.4.)

### Cancelling before flipping

In $\dfrac{3}{5}\div\dfrac{3}{7}$, do not cancel the two $3$s yet — they are not in a
product until the divisor is flipped.

## Class Practice 1: Multiply With Signs

**Problem.** Write $\left(-\dfrac{5}{6}\right)\times\dfrac{7}{11}\times\left(-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)$
as a single fraction without multiplying out.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Two negative factors, so the result is positive:

$$\frac{5\times7\times2}{6\times11\times3}$$

</details>

## Class Practice 2: Divide

**Problem.** Rewrite $\dfrac{9}{4}\div\left(-\dfrac{7}{5}\right)$ as a single fraction.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Flip the divisor and keep the negative sign:

$$-\frac{9\times5}{4\times7}$$

</details>

## Class Practice 3: Reduce

**Problem.** Simplify $\dfrac{7}{10}\times\dfrac{4}{7}\times\dfrac{10}{13}$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$\frac{7\times4\times10}{10\times7\times13}=\frac{4}{13}$$

</details>

## Class Practice 4: Fraction Over Fraction

**Problem.** Simplify $\dfrac{\quad\dfrac{3}{8}\quad}{\quad\dfrac{3}{11}\quad}$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$\frac{3}{8}\times\frac{11}{3}=\frac{3\times11}{8\times3}=\frac{11}{8}$$

</details>

## Class Practice 5: Everything Together

**Problem.** Simplify $\left(-\dfrac{6}{11}\right)\times\dfrac{4}{5}\div\left(-\dfrac{6}{5}\right)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Flip the divisor:

$$\left(-\frac{6}{11}\right)\times\frac{4}{5}\times\left(-\frac{5}{6}\right)$$

Two negatives give a positive:

$$\frac{6\times4\times5}{11\times5\times6}=\frac{4}{11}$$

</details>

## Key Takeaways

- Multiply fractions top times top and bottom times bottom; settle the sign first.
- Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its reciprocal — flip only the divisor.
- A fraction over a fraction is a division.
- Cancel a common factor between any numerator and any denominator *before* multiplying.
- Cancelling works only for products, never across $+$ or $-$.
