# Course 1 Syllabus
*Fundamental Math / Course 1*

A 36-week, 6th-grade syllabus covering the five units below, each anchored by an essential
question that frames why the unit's skills matter.

## Unit 1: Ratios and Proportional Relationships (Weeks 1–7)

**Core Focus:** Analyzing multiplicative relationships between two quantities and applying
them to real-world scaling problems.

- Weeks 1–2: Understanding the concept of ratios and using ratio language.
- Weeks 3–5: Unit rates, equivalent ratios, and ratio tables.
- Weeks 6–7: Solving real-world problems involving percentages, scaling, and unit
  conversions.

**Essential Question:** How can ratios and rates help us compare and scale quantities in
the real world?

## Unit 2: The Number System (Weeks 8–15)

**Core Focus:** Extending the number system to fractions, decimals, and negative numbers,
and locating them on the number line and coordinate plane.

- Weeks 8–10: Fractions & Decimals — fluency in multi-digit division, and multiplying and
  dividing fractions by fractions.
- Weeks 11–13: Rational Numbers — introduction to integers, negative numbers, absolute
  value, and ordering rational numbers on a number line.
- Weeks 14–15: Coordinate Plane — plotting points in all four quadrants using ordered
  pairs.

**Essential Question:** How does extending numbers beyond zero and beyond whole numbers
help us describe the world more precisely?

## Unit 3: Expressions, Equations, and Inequalities (Weeks 16–23)

**Core Focus:** Building algebraic fluency by writing, evaluating, and solving expressions,
equations, and inequalities.

- Weeks 16–18: Writing and evaluating numerical and algebraic expressions (exponents,
  order of operations, the distributive property).
- Weeks 19–21: Solving one-step equations and basic inequalities involving addition,
  subtraction, multiplication, and division.
- Weeks 22–23: Dependent and independent variables in real-world contexts.

**Essential Question:** How can we use variables and expressions to represent and solve
real-world relationships?

## Unit 4: Geometry (Weeks 24–29)

**Core Focus:** Measuring two- and three-dimensional figures by composing, decomposing, and
unfolding shapes.

- Weeks 24–25: Finding the area of triangles, special quadrilaterals, and polygons by
  composing or decomposing shapes.
- Weeks 26–27: Volume of right rectangular prisms with fractional edge lengths.
- Weeks 28–29: Surface area using 3D nets.

**Essential Question:** How does breaking shapes into simpler pieces help us measure area,
volume, and surface area?

## Unit 5: Statistics and Probability (Weeks 30–36)

**Core Focus:** Using measures of center, variability, and data displays to summarize and
interpret data sets.

- Weeks 30–31: Developing statistical thinking — understanding variability and
  distribution.
- Weeks 32–34: Calculating measures of center (mean, median) and variability (mean
  absolute deviation, interquartile range).
- Weeks 35–36: Displaying data using dot plots, histograms, and box plots.

**Essential Question:** How can we use data and its spread to describe and compare groups?
