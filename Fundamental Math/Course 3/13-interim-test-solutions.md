## Part A: Real Numbers, Decimals, and Exponents

### Problem 1 (6 points)

1. $\sqrt{81}=9$, an integer, so it is **rational**.
2. $-\dfrac{7}{12}$ is a ratio of integers with a nonzero denominator, so it is
   **rational**.
3. $0.\overline{36}$ is a repeating decimal, so it is **rational**.
4. $\sqrt{18}=3\sqrt2$. Since $18$ is not a perfect square, $\sqrt{18}$ is
   **irrational**.

Award 1 point for each correct classification and 0.5 point for each valid reason.

### Problem 2 (6 points)

1. Scale the denominator to $1000$:

   $$\frac{7}{40}=\frac{175}{1000}=0.175.$$

2. Let $x=0.1\overline6=0.1666\ldots$. Then

   $$10x=1.6666\ldots$$

   Subtracting $x$ gives $9x=1.5$, so $x=\frac{1.5}{9}=\frac16$.

Award 3 points per part: 1 for a sound setup and 2 for the correct simplified result.

### Problem 3 (8 points)

1. Apply the power to both factors, then subtract exponents when dividing:

   $$
   \frac{(2a^3b^{-2})^2}{4ab^{-1}}
   =\frac{4a^6b^{-4}}{4ab^{-1}}
   =a^5b^{-3}
   =\boxed{\frac{a^5}{b^3}}.
   $$

2. Since $81=3^4$,

   $$81^{\frac34}=(3^4)^{\frac34}=3^3=\boxed{27}. $$

Award 4 points per part. In part 1, deduct at most 1 point if the answer is left as
$a^5b^{-3}$ after otherwise correct work.

### Problem 4 (8 points)

$$
3^0=1,\qquad 2^{-2}=\frac14,\qquad \left(\frac14\right)^{-1}=4.
$$

Therefore,

$$
\frac{3^0+2^{-2}}{\left(\frac14\right)^{-1}}
=\frac{1+\frac14}{4}
=\frac{\frac54}{4}
=\boxed{\frac{5}{16}}.
$$

Award 2 points for each of the three exponent evaluations and 2 points for combining the
fractions correctly.

## Part B: Scientific Notation

### Problem 5 (5 points)

Move the decimal four places right to make a number between 1 and 10. Compensate with a
power of $10^{-4}$:

$$0.000\,728=\boxed{7.28\times10^{-4}}.$$

Award 2 points for the coefficient and 3 points for the exponent and proper notation.

### Problem 6 (7 points)

First write both terms with the same power of 10:

$$3.7\times10^4=0.37\times10^5.$$

Then add the coefficients:

$$
(6.4+0.37)\times10^5=\boxed{6.77\times10^5}.
$$

Award 3 points for aligning exponents, 2 points for the coefficient sum, and 2 points for
proper scientific notation.

## Part C: Single-Variable Linear Equations

### Problem 7 (8 points)

Distribute before collecting like terms:

$$
\begin{aligned}
4(2x-3)-5&=3(x+4)+2x\\
8x-12-5&=3x+12+2x\\
8x-17&=5x+12\\
3x&=29\\
x&=\boxed{\frac{29}{3}}.
\end{aligned}
$$

### Problem 8 (7 points)

$$
5(2x-1)-3x=7x-5
\implies 10x-5-3x=7x-5
\implies 7x-5=7x-5.
$$

Both sides are identical, so every real number satisfies the equation. There are
**infinitely many solutions**.

### Problem 9 (5 points)

For there to be no solution, the variable terms must cancel but the constants must still
disagree. Match the coefficient of $x$ on both sides:

$$k-2=3\implies k=5.$$

This would make the equation $3x+5=3x-8$, which reduces to $5=-8$. Since that is false,
the equation has no solution.

Thus $\boxed{k=5}$. Award 2 points for matching the coefficients, 1 point for solving for
$k$, and 2 points for explaining why the unequal constants produce no solution.

## Part D: Systems of Linear Equations

### Problem 10 (8 points)

Substitute $y=3x-8$ into the other equation:

$$
2x+(3x-8)=12\implies5x=20\implies x=4.
$$

Then $y=3(4)-8=4$, so the solution is $\boxed{(4,4)}$.

### Problem 11 (8 points)

Eliminate $y$ by multiplying the first equation by 3 and the second by 2:

$$
\begin{aligned}
9x+6y&=21\\
8x-6y&=64\\ \hline
17x&=85
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $x=5$. Back-substitute into $3x+2y=7$:

$$15+2y=7\implies y=-4. $$

The solution is $\boxed{(5,-4)}$.

### Problem 12 (12 points)

Eliminate $y$ by multiplying the first equation by 2:

$$
\begin{aligned}
10x-4y&=4\\
3x+4y&=22\\ \hline
13x&=26
\end{aligned}
$$

Thus $x=2$. Substitute into $5x-2y=2$:

$$5(2)-2y=2\implies -2y=-8\implies y=4.$$

The solution is $\boxed{(2,4)}$.

## Part E: Line Equations on the Coordinate Plane

### Problem 13 (8 points)

$$m=\frac{-4-5}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-9}{6}=-\frac32.$$

Use $(-2,5)$ in $y=mx+b$:

$$5=-\frac32(-2)+b=3+b,$$

so $b=2$. The equation is $\boxed{y=-\frac32x+2}$.

### Problem 14 (7 points)

For the $x$-intercept, set $y=0$: $2x=12$, so it is $(6,0)$. For the $y$-intercept, set
$x=0$: $3y=12$, so it is $(0,4)$.

Solving for $y$ gives

$$3y=-2x+12\implies\boxed{y=-\frac23x+4}. $$

### Problem 15 (9 points)

The starting fee is 18 dollars and each hour adds 7 dollars, so

$$\boxed{C=7h+18}. $$

For five hours,

$$C=7(5)+18=\boxed{53},$$

so the cost is $\boxed{\$53}$. The slope, 7, means the shop charges $\$7 for each hour.
The $C$-intercept is $(0,18)$; it is the $\$18 setup fee when no hours have been rented.

Award 3 points for the equation, 2 for the five-hour calculation, and 4 for interpreting
both the slope and intercept in context.
