# Homework 2: Lesson 16 — Comprehensive Line-Equation Practice
*Fundamental Math / Course 3*

This cumulative homework reviews the main ways we describe and use a line. Show the
calculation that supports every answer before opening a solution. Write intercepts as
ordered pairs, and keep fractions exact unless a decimal is requested.

## Part A: Is the Point on the Line?

For each point, substitute both coordinates into the equation. State whether it lies on
the line.

### Problem 1

For the line $y=2x+1$, determine whether each point lies on the line:

$$(3,7),\quad (0,1),\quad (-2,-3),\quad (4,10).$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$\begin{aligned}
(3,7)&:\quad 2(3)+1=7 &&\text{yes},\\
(0,1)&:\quad 2(0)+1=1 &&\text{yes},\\
(-2,-3)&:\quad 2(-2)+1=-3 &&\text{yes},\\
(4,10)&:\quad 2(4)+1=9\ne10 &&\text{no}.
\end{aligned}$$

Thus $\boxed{(3,7),(0,1),\text{ and }(-2,-3)}$ lie on the line, while
$\boxed{(4,10)}$ does not.

</details>

### Problem 2

For the line $3x-y=-11$, determine whether each point lies on the line:

$$(-2,5),\quad (1,14),\quad (0,11),\quad (3,19).$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$\begin{aligned}
(-2,5)&:\quad 3(-2)-5=-11 &&\text{yes},\\
(1,14)&:\quad 3(1)-14=-11 &&\text{yes},\\
(0,11)&:\quad 3(0)-11=-11 &&\text{yes},\\
(3,19)&:\quad 3(3)-19=-10\ne-11 &&\text{no}.
\end{aligned}$$

Thus $\boxed{(-2,5),(1,14),\text{ and }(0,11)}$ lie on the line, while
$\boxed{(3,19)}$ does not.

</details>

### Problem 3

For the line $x+2y=3$, determine whether each point lies on the line:

$$(1,1),\quad (3,0),\quad (5,-1),\quad (-1,2),\quad (4,-1).$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$\begin{aligned}
(1,1)&:\quad 1+2(1)=3 &&\text{yes},\\
(3,0)&:\quad 3+2(0)=3 &&\text{yes},\\
(5,-1)&:\quad 5+2(-1)=3 &&\text{yes},\\
(-1,2)&:\quad -1+2(2)=3 &&\text{yes},\\
(4,-1)&:\quad 4+2(-1)=2\ne3 &&\text{no}.
\end{aligned}$$

Thus the first four points lie on the line, and $\boxed{(4,-1)}$ does not.

</details>

## Part C: Slope and Intercepts

### Problem 6

Find the slope of the line through $(-4,6)$ and $(2,-3)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$m=\frac{-3-6}{2-(-4)}=\frac{-9}{6}=-\frac32.$$

The slope is $\boxed{-\frac32}$.

</details>

### Problem 7

Find the slope of the line through $(5,-1)$ and $(5,9)$. What kind of line is it?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$m=\frac{9-(-1)}{5-5}=\frac{10}{0}.$$

Division by zero is undefined. The line is vertical, so its slope is
$\boxed{\text{undefined}}$ and the line is $\boxed{x=5}$.

</details>

### Problem 8

For $2x+3y=12$, find the slope, the $y$-intercept, and the $x$-intercept.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Solve for $y$:

$$3y=-2x+12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\frac23x+4.$$

Thus the slope is $\boxed{-\frac23}$ and the $y$-intercept is $\boxed{(0,4)}$.
For the $x$-intercept, set $y=0$: $2x=12$, so $x=6$. The $x$-intercept is
$\boxed{(6,0)}$.

</details>

### Problem 9

For $x=-3$, state the slope, the $y$-intercept, and the $x$-intercept.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

This is a vertical line. Its slope is $\boxed{\text{undefined}}$. It never has $x=0$,
so it has $\boxed{\text{no }y\text{-intercept}}$. Every point on it has $x=-3$; when
$y=0$, the point is $\boxed{(-3,0)}$, its $x$-intercept.

</details>

## Part D: Write Equations of Lines

Write each equation in slope-intercept form when possible.

### Problem 10

Write the equation of the line with slope $4$ through $(-2,5)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Use point-slope form:

$$y-5=4\bigl(x-(-2)\bigr)=4(x+2).$$

Simplifying gives $y-5=4x+8$, so the equation is $\boxed{y=4x+13}$.

</details>

### Problem 11

Write the equation of the line with slope $-\frac12$ through $(6,-4)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$y+4=-\frac12(x-6).$$

Distributing gives $y+4=-\frac12x+3$, so the equation is
$\boxed{y=-\frac12x-7}$.

</details>

### Problem 12

Write the equation of the horizontal line through $(7,3)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

A horizontal line has the same $y$-value at every point. Its equation is
$\boxed{y=3}$.

</details>

### Problem 13

Write the equation of the vertical line through $(-5,2)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

A vertical line has the same $x$-value at every point. Its equation is
$\boxed{x=-5}$.

</details>

## Part E: Intersections of Two Lines

Find each intersection. Check your ordered pair in both equations.

### Problem 14

Find the intersection of $y=3x-2$ and $y=-x+10$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

At the intersection, the two expressions for $y$ are equal:

$$3x-2=-x+10\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 4x=12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=3.$$

Then $y=3(3)-2=7$. The intersection is $\boxed{(3,7)}$.

Check: $-3+10=7$, so the point satisfies both equations.

</details>

### Problem 15

Find the intersection of $2x+y=11$ and $x-2y=-7$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

From the first equation, $y=11-2x$. Substitute into the second:

$$x-2(11-2x)=-7\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 5x=15\quad\Longrightarrow\quad x=3.$$

Then $y=11-2(3)=5$. The intersection is $\boxed{(3,5)}$.

Check: $2(3)+5=11$ and $3-2(5)=-7$.

</details>
