# Lesson 15: Practice Review — Line Equations
*Fundamental Math / Course 3*

This is a guided review of the line-equation skills from Lessons 12 and 14. Work each
problem first, show the algebra that supports your answer, and then compare with the
solution. When a problem asks for a line equation, an equivalent equation is acceptable
unless a particular form is requested.

## Learning Targets

By the end of class, you should be able to:

- calculate slope from two points and interpret it as a rate of change;
- use a point and a slope to find an unknown coordinate;
- write equations of lines from points, slopes, intercepts, and standard form;
- find and interpret the intersection of two lines; and
- build and use line equations in word problems.

## Quick Reference

For points $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$,

$$m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}.$$

For a nonvertical line with slope $m$ through $(x_0,y_0)$,

$$y-y_0=m(x-x_0), \qquad \text{or} \qquad y=m(x-x_0)+y_0.$$

In slope-intercept form $y=mx+b$, $m$ is the slope and $(0,b)$ is the $y$-intercept.
Horizontal lines have the form $y=k$ and slope $0$. Vertical lines have the form $x=k$
and an undefined slope.

## Part A: Slope From Two Points

Keep the order of subtraction matched: if the numerator is $y_2-y_1$, the denominator
must be $x_2-x_1$.

### Problem 1

Find the slope of the line through $(-3,5)$ and $(1,-7)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$m=\frac{-7-5}{1-(-3)}=\frac{-12}{4}=\boxed{-3}.$$

</details>

### Problem 2

Find the slope of the line through $(4,-2)$ and $(4,9)$. Then write its equation.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The $x$-coordinates are the same, so the run is $4-4=0$. The slope is undefined, and
the line is vertical. Its equation is $\boxed{x=4}$.

</details>

### Problem 3

A line passes through $(2,1)$ and $(8,1)$. Find its slope and equation.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$m=\frac{1-1}{8-2}=0.$$

The line is horizontal through height $1$, so its equation is $\boxed{y=1}$.

</details>

## Part B: Use a Slope and a Point

The slope tells how the $y$-value changes for a change in $x$. You can either use
$y-y_0=m(x-x_0)$ or reason directly with rise over run.

### Problem 4

A line has slope $\frac34$ and passes through $(2,-1)$. What is the $y$-coordinate of the
point on the line whose $x$-coordinate is $10$?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The change in $x$ is $10-2=8$. Therefore the change in $y$ is

$$\frac34\cdot 8=6.$$

Starting at $y=-1$, the new $y$-coordinate is $-1+6=\boxed{5}$. The point is $(10,5)$.

</details>

### Problem 5

The point $(6,11)$ lies on a line with slope $-2$. Find the point on the line with
$x=9$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Here $\Delta x=9-6=3$, so $\Delta y=(-2)(3)=-6$. Thus $y=11-6=5$, and the point is
$\boxed{(9,5)}$.

</details>

### Problem 6

Line $L$ passes through $(-2,4)$ and has slope $-\frac12$. Does $(6,0)$ lie on $L$?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

From $x=-2$ to $x=6$, the change in $x$ is $8$. The corresponding change in $y$ should be

$$-\frac12(8)=-4.$$

Starting at $4$ gives $4+(-4)=0$, so $\boxed{(6,0)\text{ lies on }L}$.

</details>

## Part C: Write the Equation of a Line

### Problem 7

Write the equation of the line with slope $5$ through $(-1,2)$ in slope-intercept form.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$$y-2=5(x-(-1))=5(x+1)$$

$$y-2=5x+5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{y=5x+7}.$$

Checking $x=-1$ gives $y=2$.

</details>

### Problem 8

Write an equation for the line through $(3,-2)$ and $(7,10)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

First calculate the slope:

$$m=\frac{10-(-2)}{7-3}=\frac{12}{4}=3.$$

Use $(3,-2)$:

$$y+2=3(x-3)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y+2=3x-9\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\boxed{y=3x-11}.$$

</details>

### Problem 9

Write an equation for the line through $(-4,6)$ and $(2,6)$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Both points have $y=6$, so the line is horizontal. Its equation is $\boxed{y=6}$.

</details>

## Exit Ticket

Without looking back, answer these three questions.

1. Find the slope through $(-2,3)$ and $(4,-9)$.
2. Write an equation for the line with slope $-2$ through $(3,7)$.
3. Find the intersection of $y=x+6$ and $y=-2x$.

<details><summary>Answers</summary>

1. $\displaystyle \frac{-9-3}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-12}{6}=\boxed{-2}$.
2. $y-7=-2(x-3)$, so $\boxed{y=-2x+13}$.
3. $x+6=-2x$, so $x=-2$ and $y=4$: $\boxed{(-2,4)}$.

</details>
