*Fundamental Math / Course 3*

Show the slope calculation or equation rearrangement that supports every answer. When
writing a line equation, an equivalent equation is acceptable unless a form is requested.
For a vertical line, use an equation of the form $x=k$ rather than slope-intercept form.
Try each problem before opening its solution.

## Part A: Identify the Relationship

For Problems 1–6, decide whether the pair of lines is **parallel**, the **same line**,
or **intersecting**. Support your conclusion with slopes or equivalent equations.

**Problem 1.** $y=\frac25x+3$ and $y=\frac25x-6$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Both lines have slope $\frac25$, but their $y$-intercepts are different. They are
distinct lines with the same slope, so they are $\boxed{\text{parallel}}$.

</details>

**Problem 2.** $3x+4y=12$ and $y=-\frac34x+3$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Rearrange the first equation:

$$3x+4y=12\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\frac34x+3.$$

This is exactly the second equation, so the pair is $\boxed{\text{the same line}}$.

</details>

**Problem 3.** $2x-y=5$ and $y=-2x+1$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The first equation becomes $y=2x-5$, whose slope is $2$. The second slope is $-2$.
Different slopes mean the lines are $\boxed{\text{intersecting}}$.

</details>

**Problem 4.** $x=-7$ and $x=2$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Both are vertical lines, and they have different $x$-values. Therefore, they are
$\boxed{\text{parallel}}$.

</details>

**Problem 5.** $y=0$ and $4x-2y=8$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The first line is horizontal and has slope $0$. The second equation becomes
$y=2x-4$, which has slope $2$. The lines are $\boxed{\text{intersecting}}$.

</details>

**Problem 6.** $6x-9y=15$ and $2x-3y=5$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Dividing the first equation by $3$ gives $2x-3y=5$, exactly the second equation.
They are $\boxed{\text{the same line}}$.

</details>

## Part B: Use Points and Slopes

**Problem 7.** Find the slope of the line through $(-3,4)$ and $(5,-8)$. Then write an
equation of the line through $(2,7)$ parallel to it in slope-intercept form.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The original slope is

$$m=\frac{-8-4}{5-(-3)}=\frac{-12}{8}=-\frac32.$$

The parallel line has the same slope. Using $(2,7)$,

$$y-7=-\frac32(x-2).$$

Thus $y-7=-\frac32x+3$, so the equation is
$\boxed{y=-\frac32x+10}$.

</details>

**Problem 8.** Write an equation in point-slope form for the line through $(-4,-1)$
parallel to $5x+2y=14$. Then write it in slope-intercept form.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

First, $5x+2y=14$ becomes $y=-\frac52x+7$, so the required slope is
$-\frac52$. Point-slope form is

$$\boxed{y+1=-\frac52(x+4)}.$$

Expanding gives $y+1=-\frac52x-10$, so slope-intercept form is

$$\boxed{y=-\frac52x-11}.$$

</details>

**Problem 9.** Write an equation of the line through $(8,3)$ parallel to $y=-4$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The given line is horizontal, so a parallel line through $(8,3)$ is also horizontal.
Its equation is $\boxed{y=3}$.

</details>

**Problem 10.** Write an equation of the line through $(-5,6)$ parallel to $x=1$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The given line is vertical, so the parallel line is vertical. Passing through
$(-5,6)$ means its equation is $\boxed{x=-5}$.

</details>

## Part C: Work From Standard Form

**Problem 11.** Determine whether $4x+6y=18$ and $2x+3y=-9$ are parallel, the same
line, or intersecting.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Both equations have slope $-\frac23$:

$$4x+6y=18\Longrightarrow y=-\frac23x+3,$$

$$2x+3y=-9\Longrightarrow y=-\frac23x-3.$$

The intercepts differ, so the lines are distinct and $\boxed{\text{parallel}}$.

</details>

**Problem 12.** Find $k$ so that the line $3x+ky=12$ is parallel to
$6x-4y=9$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The slope of $6x-4y=9$ is

$$-\frac{6}{-4}=\frac32.$$

The slope of $3x+ky=12$ is $-\frac3k$. Set the slopes equal:

$$-\frac3k=\frac32.$$

Multiplying by $2k$ gives $-6=3k$, so $\boxed{k=-2}$. The resulting line is
$3x-2y=12$, which has slope $\frac32$ and a different intercept from the given line.

</details>

## Part D: Explain and Apply

**Problem 13.** Line $L$ has equation $y=\frac13x+2$. A student says that
$y=\frac13x+2$ is an equation of a line parallel to $L$. Is the student correct?
Explain, then write an equation of a line through $(0,-4)$ parallel to $L$.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The student is not correct: the proposed equation is exactly $L$, not a distinct
parallel line. A line through $(0,-4)$ with the same slope $\frac13$ is

$$\boxed{y=\frac13x-4}.$$

It has the same slope as $L$ but a different $y$-intercept.

</details>

**Problem 14.** Two roads are modeled by $y=-\frac12x+9$ and
$2x+4y=20$, where $x$ and $y$ are distances in kilometers on a map. Do the roads meet?
If not, explain what their relationship is. Then write an equation for a road through
$(0,1)$ that is parallel to them.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Rewrite the second road:

$$2x+4y=20\quad\Longrightarrow\quad y=-\frac12x+5.$$

Both roads have slope $-\frac12$ but different $y$-intercepts, so they are distinct
parallel roads and do not meet. A road through $(0,1)$ with this slope is

$$\boxed{y=-\frac12x+1}.$$

</details>
