# Homework: Lessons 7–8 — Scientific Notation and Linear Equations
*Fundamental Math / Course 3*

This homework covers [07-scientific-notation.md](./07-scientific-notation.md) (writing
numbers as $a \times 10^n$, converting to and from standard form, and multiplying, dividing,
adding, and subtracting in scientific notation) and
[08-single-variable-linear-equations.md](./08-single-variable-linear-equations.md) (the
distributive property, combining like terms, variables on both sides, and word problems).

## Part A: Converting To and From Scientific Notation

### Problem 1

Write $3{,}450{,}000$ in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The decimal point moves $6$ places left to land after the first nonzero digit:

$$3{,}450{,}000 = 3.45 \times 10^6$$

The answer is **$3.45 \times 10^6$**.

</details>

### Problem 2

Write $0.000067$ in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The decimal point moves $5$ places right to land after the first nonzero digit, giving a
negative exponent:

$$0.000067 = 6.7 \times 10^{-5}$$

The answer is **$6.7 \times 10^{-5}$**.

</details>

### Problem 3

Write $4.02 \times 10^{4}$ in standard form.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

A positive exponent moves the decimal point $4$ places right:

$$4.02 \times 10^{4} = 40{,}200$$

The answer is **$40{,}200$**.

</details>

### Problem 4

Write $9.1 \times 10^{-6}$ in standard form.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

A negative exponent moves the decimal point $6$ places left:

$$9.1 \times 10^{-6} = 0.0000091$$

The answer is **$0.0000091$**.

</details>

### Problem 5

Rewrite $52 \times 10^{3}$ in proper scientific notation (with $1 \le a < 10$).

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$52$ is not less than $10$, so shift its decimal point one place left (dividing $a$ by $10$)
and raise the exponent by $1$ to compensate:

$$52 \times 10^3 = 5.2 \times 10^1 \times 10^3 = 5.2 \times 10^{1+3} = 5.2 \times 10^4$$

The answer is **$5.2 \times 10^4$**.

</details>

## Part B: Multiplying and Dividing in Scientific Notation

### Problem 6

Compute $(2 \times 10^3) \times (3 \times 10^5)$, giving the answer in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply the $a$-parts, add the exponents:

$$(2 \times 10^3) \times (3 \times 10^5) = (2 \times 3) \times 10^{3+5} = 6 \times 10^8$$

The answer is **$6 \times 10^8$**.

</details>

### Problem 7

Compute $(7 \times 10^{-2}) \times (6 \times 10^{4})$, giving the answer in scientific
notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply the $a$-parts, add the exponents:

$$(7 \times 10^{-2}) \times (6 \times 10^{4}) = (7 \times 6) \times 10^{-2+4} = 42 \times 10^2$$

$42$ is not less than $10$, so renormalize:

$$42 \times 10^2 = 4.2 \times 10^1 \times 10^2 = 4.2 \times 10^3$$

The answer is **$4.2 \times 10^3$**.

</details>

### Problem 8

Compute $(8.4 \times 10^{6}) \div (2 \times 10^{2})$, giving the answer in scientific
notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Divide the $a$-parts, subtract the exponents:

$$(8.4 \times 10^6) \div (2 \times 10^2) = (8.4 \div 2) \times 10^{6-2} = 4.2 \times 10^4$$

The answer is **$4.2 \times 10^4$**.

</details>

### Problem 9

Compute $(5 \times 10^{-3}) \div (2 \times 10^{4})$, giving the answer in scientific
notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Divide the $a$-parts, subtract the exponents:

$$(5 \times 10^{-3}) \div (2 \times 10^{4}) = (5 \div 2) \times 10^{-3-4} = 2.5 \times 10^{-7}$$

The answer is **$2.5 \times 10^{-7}$**.

</details>

## Part C: Adding, Subtracting, and Real-World Comparisons

### Problem 10

Compute $4.1 \times 10^5 + 2.6 \times 10^5$, giving the answer in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Same exponent already, so add the $a$-parts directly:

$$4.1 \times 10^5 + 2.6 \times 10^5 = (4.1 + 2.6) \times 10^5 = 6.7 \times 10^5$$

The answer is **$6.7 \times 10^5$**.

</details>

### Problem 11

Compute $7 \times 10^{6} - 4 \times 10^{5}$, giving the answer in scientific notation.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Rewrite $4 \times 10^5$ with exponent $6$: shifting its decimal point left $1$ place raises
the exponent from $5$ to $6$.

$$4 \times 10^5 = 0.4 \times 10^6$$

Subtract the $a$-parts:

$$7 \times 10^6 - 0.4 \times 10^6 = (7 - 0.4) \times 10^6 = 6.6 \times 10^6$$

The answer is **$6.6 \times 10^6$**.

</details>

### Problem 12

A virus is about $1 \times 10^{-7}$ meters across. A bacterium is about $2 \times 10^{-6}$
meters across. About how many times wider is the bacterium than the virus?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

"How many times wider" is a division:

$$\frac{2 \times 10^{-6}}{1 \times 10^{-7}} = (2 \div 1) \times 10^{-6-(-7)} = 2 \times 10^{1} = 20$$

The answer is **$20$** — the bacterium is about $20$ times wider than the virus.

</details>

## Part D: Distributive Property and Combining Like Terms

### Problem 13

Simplify $4(x + 3) + 2x$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Distribute the $4$, then combine like terms:

$$4(x + 3) + 2x = 4x + 12 + 2x = (4x + 2x) + 12 = 6x + 12$$

The answer is **$6x + 12$**.

</details>

### Problem 14

Solve $5(x - 2) + 3 = 18$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Distribute, then combine like terms on the left:

$$5(x - 2) + 3 = 5x - 10 + 3 = 5x - 7$$

So the equation is $5x - 7 = 18$. Undo subtraction, then undo multiplication:

$$5x = 25 \implies x = 5$$

Check: $5(5 - 2) + 3 = 5(3) + 3 = 15 + 3 = 18$. $\checkmark$

The answer is **$x = 5$**.

</details>

## Part E: Variables on Both Sides

### Problem 15

Solve $6x + 5 = 3x + 20$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Subtract $3x$ from both sides to collect $x$ terms on the left:

$$6x - 3x + 5 = 3x - 3x + 20 \implies 3x + 5 = 20$$

Undo addition, then undo multiplication:

$$3x = 15 \implies x = 5$$

Check: $6(5) + 5 = 35$ and $3(5) + 20 = 35$. $\checkmark$

The answer is **$x = 5$**.

</details>

### Problem 16

Solve $3(x + 4) = 2x + 17$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Distribute the left side first:

$$3x + 12 = 2x + 17$$

Subtract $2x$ from both sides:

$$x + 12 = 17 \implies x = 5$$

Check: $3(5 + 4) = 3(9) = 27$ and $2(5) + 17 = 27$. $\checkmark$

The answer is **$x = 5$**.

</details>

### Problem 17

Without fully solving, determine whether $5x + 4(x - 1) = 9x + 4$ has one solution, no
solution, or infinitely many solutions.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Simplify the left side:

$$5x + 4(x - 1) = 5x + 4x - 4 = 9x - 4$$

The equation is now $9x - 4 = 9x + 4$. Subtract $9x$ from both sides:

$$-4 = 4$$

This is false for every $x$, so there is **no solution**.

</details>

## Part F: Word Problems

### Problem 18

Find three consecutive integers whose sum is $72$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let the first integer be $n$; the next two are $n+1$ and $n+2$:

$$n + (n+1) + (n+2) = 72$$

Combine like terms, then solve:

$$3n + 3 = 72 \implies 3n = 69 \implies n = 23$$

The three integers are $23$, $24$, $25$.

The answer is **$23, 24, 25$**.

</details>

### Problem 19

Maya has $x$ dollars. Noah has $5$ dollars less than twice what Maya has. Combined, they
have the same amount as $5$ times what Maya has, minus $20$ dollars. How much money does
Maya have?

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Maya has $x$. Noah has "$5$ less than twice Maya's amount":

$$\text{Noah} = 2x - 5$$

"Combined" is addition; the right side is "$5$ times Maya's amount, minus $20$":

$$x + (2x - 5) = 5x - 20$$

Combine like terms on the left, then move $x$ terms to one side:

$$3x - 5 = 5x - 20$$

$$-5 + 20 = 5x - 3x \implies 15 = 2x \implies x = 7.5$$

Check: Maya has $7.5$, Noah has $2(7.5) - 5 = 10$, combined $= 17.5$; and
$5(7.5) - 20 = 17.5$. $\checkmark$

The answer is **Maya has \$7.50**.

</details>
