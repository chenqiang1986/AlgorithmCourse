# Homework: Lesson 14 — Factoring Quadratic Equations
*Fundamental Math / Algebra I*

This homework covers [Lesson 14](./14-factoring-quadratic-equations.md): using the
zero-product property to solve factored equations, factoring monic and non-monic
quadratics, factoring out a GCF, recognizing special patterns, and connecting roots to
$x$-intercepts. Show the factoring step before solving each equation.

## Part A: Read Factors and Roots

### Problem 1

Solve $(x-8)(x+3)=0$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Set each factor equal to zero:

$$x-8=0\quad\text{or}\quad x+3=0.$$

Therefore, $\boxed{x=8\text{ or }x=-3}$.

</details>

### Problem 2

The function $f(x)=(x+5)(x-2)$ is zero at two values of $x$. Find the roots and state
the $x$-intercepts of its graph.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$$
(x+5)(x-2)=0
\implies x=-5\text{ or }x=2.
$$

The roots are $\boxed{-5\text{ and }2}$, so the $x$-intercepts are
$\boxed{(-5,0)\text{ and }(2,0)}$.

</details>

### Problem 3

Solve $4(x-1)(2x+7)=0$.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The factor $4$ is nonzero, so solve the variable-containing factors:

$$
x-1=0\quad\text{or}\quad 2x+7=0.
$$

Thus $\boxed{x=1\text{ or }x=-\frac72}$.

</details>

## Part B: Factor Monic Trinomials

### Problem 4

Solve $x^2+9x+20=0$ by factoring.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The numbers $4$ and $5$ have product $20$ and sum $9$:

$$
x^2+9x+20=(x+4)(x+5).
$$

So $(x+4)(x+5)=0$, giving $\boxed{x=-4\text{ or }x=-5}$.

</details>

### Problem 5

Solve $x^2-2x-24=0$ by factoring.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The numbers $-6$ and $4$ have product $-24$ and sum $-2$:

$$
x^2-2x-24=(x-6)(x+4).
$$

Thus $\boxed{x=6\text{ or }x=-4}$.

</details>

### Problem 6

Solve $x^2+3x=28$ by factoring.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

First put zero on one side:

$$x^2+3x-28=0.$$

The factors are $(x+7)(x-4)$:

$$
(x+7)(x-4)=0.
$$

Therefore, $\boxed{x=-7\text{ or }x=4}$.

</details>

## Part C: Leading Coefficients, GCFs, and Patterns

### Problem 7

Solve $2x^2-7x+3=0$ by factoring.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$$
2x^2-7x+3=(2x-1)(x-3).
$$

Therefore,

$$2x-1=0\quad\text{or}\quad x-3=0,$$

so $\boxed{x=\frac12\text{ or }x=3}$.

</details>

### Problem 8

Solve $3x^2+11x+6=0$ by factoring.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$$
3x^2+11x+6=(3x+2)(x+3).
$$

Thus $3x+2=0$ or $x+3=0$, so

$$\boxed{x=-\frac23\text{ or }x=-3}.$$

</details>

### Problem 9

Solve $5x^2-20x=0$ by factoring out the GCF first.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Factor out $5x$:

$$
5x^2-20x=5x(x-4).
$$

Then $5x(x-4)=0$, so $x=0$ or $x-4=0$. The roots are

$$\boxed{x=0\text{ or }x=4}.$$

</details>

### Problem 10

Solve $16x^2-9=0$ by factoring.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

This is a difference of squares:

$$
16x^2-9=(4x-3)(4x+3).
$$

So $4x-3=0$ or $4x+3=0$. Therefore,

$$\boxed{x=\frac34\text{ or }x=-\frac34}.$$

</details>

## Part D: Repeated Roots and Reasoning

### Problem 11

Factor and solve $x^2-12x+36=0$. State whether the graph of
$y=x^2-12x+36$ crosses or touches the $x$-axis.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$$
x^2-12x+36=(x-6)^2=(x-6)(x-6).
$$

The only root is $\boxed{x=6}$, repeated twice. The graph has one $x$-intercept,
$(6,0)$, and **touches** the $x$-axis there rather than crossing it.

</details>

### Problem 12

A rectangle has side lengths $(x+2)$ and $(x-5)$. Its area is $30$ square units. Find
all real values of $x$ that solve the equation, then decide which value can describe the
rectangle.

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Start with the area equation:

$$
(x+2)(x-5)=30.
$$

Expand and put zero on one side:

$$
x^2-3x-10=30
\implies x^2-3x-40=0.
$$

Factor:

$$
(x-8)(x+5)=0.
$$

Thus $x=8$ or $x=-5$. For a rectangle, both side lengths must be positive. The value
$\boxed{x=8}$ gives side lengths $10$ and $3$, so it works. The value $x=-5$ gives
negative side lengths and cannot describe a rectangle.

</details>
