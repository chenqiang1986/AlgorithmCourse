# Homework: Lesson 16 — Two-Variable Linear Equations Review
*Fundamental Math / Algebra I*

This homework reviews two-variable linear systems. Select a sensible method, show every
algebraic step, and check each ordered pair in both original equations. For classification
problems, explain what the equations show about the lines.

## Part A: Solve by Substitution

### Problem 1

Solve the system.

$$\begin{cases}y=4x-3\\x+y=12\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$x+(4x-3)=12$ gives $5x=15$, so $x=3$ and $y=9$. The solution is $\boxed{(3,9)}$.

</details>

### Problem 2

Solve the system.

$$\begin{cases}x=2y+1\\3x-y=16\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$3(2y+1)-y=16$ gives $5y=13$, so $y=\frac{13}{5}$ and
$x=2\left(\frac{13}{5}\right)+1=\frac{31}{5}$. Thus $\boxed{\left(\frac{31}{5},\frac{13}{5}\right)}$.

</details>

### Problem 3

Solve the system.

$$\begin{cases}2x+y=1\\y=7-x\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

$2x+(7-x)=1$ gives $x=-6$. Then $y=7-(-6)=13$, so $\boxed{(-6,13)}$.

</details>

## Part B: Solve by Elimination

### Problem 4

Solve the system.

$$\begin{cases}5x+2y=16\\5x-3y=1\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Subtract the second equation from the first: $5y=15$, so $y=3$. Then
$5x-9=1$, giving $x=2$. The solution is $\boxed{(2,3)}$.

</details>

### Problem 5

Solve the system.

$$\begin{cases}3x+4y=23\\2x-3y=-4\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply the first equation by $3$ and the second by $4$:

$$9x+12y=69\qquad 8x-12y=-16.$$

Adding gives $17x=53$, so $x=\frac{53}{17}$. Then
$2\left(\frac{53}{17}\right)-3y=-4$, giving $y=\frac{58}{17}$. Thus
$\boxed{\left(\frac{53}{17},\frac{58}{17}\right)}$.

</details>

### Problem 6

Solve the system.

$$\begin{cases}7x+y=18\\3x-2y=-9\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply the first equation by $2$ and add:

$$14x+2y=36$$
$$3x-2y=-9$$
$$17x=27.$$

So $x=\frac{27}{17}$ and $y=18-7\left(\frac{27}{17}\right)=\frac{117}{17}$.
The solution is $\boxed{\left(\frac{27}{17},\frac{117}{17}\right)}$.

</details>

## Part C: Classify the System

### Problem 7

Classify the system.

$$\begin{cases}4x+y=9\\8x+2y=18\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

The second equation is twice the first, so the system has $\boxed{\text{infinitely many solutions}}$.

</details>

### Problem 8

Classify the system.

$$\begin{cases}3x-2y=7\\6x-4y=20\end{cases}$$

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Twice the first equation would be $6x-4y=14$, not $20$. The lines are parallel, so there
is $\boxed{\text{no solution}}$.

</details>

## Part D: Applications

### Problem 9

A store sells notebooks for $3$ dollars and binders for $7$ dollars. A customer buys
$18$ items and pays $94$ dollars. How many notebooks and binders did the customer buy?

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Let $n$ be notebooks and $b$ be binders:

$$n+b=18\qquad 3n+7b=94.$$

Multiply the first equation by $-3$ and add to get $4b=40$, so $b=10$ and $n=8$.
The customer bought $\boxed{8\text{ notebooks and }10\text{ binders}}$.

</details>

### Problem 10

The perimeter of a rectangle is $54$ centimeters. Its length is $5$ centimeters more
than twice its width. Find the length and width.

<details><summary>Solution</summary>

Let $l$ be length and $w$ be width. The equations are

$$2l+2w=54\qquad l=2w+5.$$

Substitute: $2(2w+5)+2w=54$, so $6w=44$ and $w=\frac{22}{3}$. Then
$l=2\left(\frac{22}{3}\right)+5=\frac{59}{3}$. The dimensions are
$\boxed{\frac{59}{3}\text{ cm by }\frac{22}{3}\text{ cm}}$.

</details>
