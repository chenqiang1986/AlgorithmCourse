# Course 2 Syllabus

A 36-week, 7th-grade syllabus covering the six units below, each anchored by an essential
question that frames why the unit's skills matter.

## Unit 1: Rational Number Operations (Weeks 1–8)

**Core Focus:** Mastering positive and negative numbers using visual models.

- Weeks 1–2: Adding and subtracting integers using number lines and counters.
- Weeks 3–4: Multiplying and dividing integers (understanding sign rules).
- Weeks 5–6: Applying operations to rational decimals and fractions.
- Weeks 7–8: Real-world multi-step problems involving rational numbers (e.g., bank
  accounts, temperature).

**Essential Question:** How do negative numbers help us describe changes in the world
around us?

## Unit 2: Ratios & Proportional Relationships (Weeks 9–15)

**Core Focus:** Analyzing multiplicative relationships between two quantities.

- Weeks 9–10: Calculating unit rates with complex fractions (e.g., miles per hour with
  fractional values).
- Weeks 11–12: Identifying proportional relationships in tables, graphs, and equations
  ($y = kx$).
- Weeks 13–14: Interpreting the constant of proportionality ($k$) as the unit rate on a
  graph ($1, r$).
- Week 15: Graphing proportional relationships and interpreting points.

**Essential Question:** What does it mean for two quantities to scale together perfectly?

## Unit 3: Percentages & Proportional Applications (Weeks 16–20)

**Core Focus:** Using proportions to solve real-world monetary and measurement problems.

- Weeks 16–17: Finding percents of numbers, percent increase, and percent decrease.
- Weeks 18–19: Solving real-world problems: tips, taxes, discounts, commissions, and
  simple interest.
- Week 20: Calculating percent error in measurements.

**Essential Question:** How does scaling numbers by 100 help us compare financial and
real-world changes?

## Unit 4: Expressions & Equations (Weeks 21–26)

**Core Focus:** Introducing formal algebraic manipulation.

- Weeks 21–22: Adding, subtracting, and factoring linear expressions with rational
  coefficients.
- Weeks 23–24: Solving two-step equations ($px + q = r$).
- Weeks 25–26: Solving and graphing simple linear inequalities ($px + q > r$).

**Essential Question:** How can we use variables to find unknown values in balance
problems?

## Unit 5: Geometry & Scale Drawings (Weeks 27–32)

**Core Focus:** Understanding shapes, scaling, and circle measurements.

- Weeks 27–28: Solving problems with scale drawings and scale factors.
- Weeks 29–30: Formulas for area and circumference of a circle.
- Weeks 31–32: Finding unknown angles (complementary, supplementary, vertical, and
  adjacent).

**Essential Question:** How does geometry help us accurately replicate the physical world
at different scales?

## Unit 6: Statistics & Probability (Weeks 33–36)

**Core Focus:** Sampling populations and predicting the likelihood of events.

- Weeks 33–34: Using random sampling to draw inferences about a population.
- Week 35: Calculating theoretical and experimental probability of single events.
- Week 36: Probability models and compound events using tree diagrams.

**Essential Question:** How can we use data and probability to make accurate predictions
about a group?
