# Algebra I Syllabus

A 36-week Algebra I syllabus covering the six units below, each anchored by an essential
question that frames why the unit's skills matter.

## Unit 1: Foundations of Algebra & Linear Equations (Weeks 1–6)

**Core Focus:** Transitioning variables from placeholders to dynamic components of
equations.

- Weeks 1–2: Algebraic properties, simplifying expressions, and absolute value operations.
- Weeks 3–4: Solving multi-step equations and literal equations (rearranging formulas for
  a specific variable).
- Weeks 5–6: Solving and graphing compound inequalities and absolute value equations.

**Essential Question:** How do we use algebraic properties to isolate an unknown variable
in a complex system?

## Unit 2: Linear Functions & Function Notation (Weeks 7–13)

**Core Focus:** Defining what a function is and mastering the three forms of linear
equations.

- Weeks 7–8: Introduction to functions: domain, range, mapping diagrams, and function
  notation $f(x)$.
- Weeks 9–10: Arithmetic sequences and finding slope/rate of change across different
  representations.
- Weeks 11–12: Graphing and writing linear equations: Slope-Intercept ($y = mx + b$),
  Point-Slope ($y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$), and Standard Form ($Ax + By = C$).
- Week 13: Graphing linear inequalities in two variables.

**Essential Question:** How does function notation help us model predictable, constant
change in the real world?

## Unit 3: Systems of Equations & Inequalities (Weeks 14–19)

**Core Focus:** Finding the intersection of multiple constraints.

- Weeks 14–15: Solving systems of linear equations by graphing and substitution.
- Weeks 16–17: Solving systems via elimination (including scaling equations).
- Week 18: Modeling real-world constraints using systems of equations word problems.
- Week 19: Graphing systems of linear inequalities (finding overlapping solution regions).

**Essential Question:** How do we determine the single point of balance when balancing
multiple conditions simultaneously?

## Unit 4: Exponents, Radicals, & Exponential Functions (Weeks 20–25)

**Core Focus:** Shifting from constant addition (linear) to constant multiplication
(exponential).

- Weeks 20–21: Rational exponents, radical properties, and simplifying radical
  expressions.
- Weeks 22–23: Geometric sequences and graphing exponential functions ($f(x) = ab^x$).
- Weeks 24–25: Modeling exponential growth and decay (e.g., compound interest, population
  dynamics).

**Essential Question:** What makes exponential growth fundamentally different from linear
growth over time?

## Unit 5: Polynomials & Factoring (Weeks 26–30)

**Core Focus:** Breaking down complex expressions into their structural components.

- Weeks 26–27: Polynomial operations: adding, subtracting, and multiplying (FOIL, area
  models).
- Week 28: Factoring out the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) and factoring by grouping.
- Weeks 29–30: Factoring quadratic trinomials ($x^2 + bx + c$ and $ax^2 + bx + c$) and
  special products (difference of squares).

**Essential Question:** How is factoring a polynomial similar to finding the prime
factorization of an integer?

## Unit 6: Quadratic Functions & Equations (Weeks 31–36)

**Core Focus:** Analyzing parabolas and mastering the multiple ways to solve a quadratic
equation.

- Weeks 31–32: Graphing quadratic functions ($f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$), identifying
  vertices, axes of symmetry, and intercepts.
- Week 33: Solving quadratics by graphing and finding square roots.
- Week 34: Solving quadratics by completing the square.
- Week 35: The Quadratic Formula
  ($x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$) and using the discriminant to check for real
  solutions.
- Week 36: Comparing linear, exponential, and quadratic models; final course review.

**Essential Question:** How do the different methods of solving a quadratic equation
expose different properties of a parabola?
