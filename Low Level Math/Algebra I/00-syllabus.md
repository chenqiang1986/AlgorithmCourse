# Algebra I Syllabus

A 30-week Algebra I syllabus covering the five units below, each anchored by an essential
question that frames why the unit's skills matter.

## Unit 1: Systems of Equations & Inequalities (Weeks 1–6)

**Core Focus:** Finding the intersection of multiple constraints.
**AOPS Algebra** Unit 5 & 9

- Weeks 1–2: Solving systems of linear equations by graphing and substitution.
- Weeks 3–4: Solving systems via elimination (including scaling equations).
- Week 5: Modeling real-world constraints using systems of equations word problems.
- Week 6: Graphing systems of linear inequalities (finding overlapping solution regions).

**Essential Question:** How do we determine the single point of balance when balancing
multiple conditions simultaneously?

## Unit 2: Foundations of Algebra & Linear Functions (Weeks 7–13)

**Core Focus:** Students already solve one-variable linear equations and inequalities, so
this unit moves quickly through the remaining algebraic-manipulation skills before
shifting to functions and the three forms of linear equations.
**AOPS Algebra** Unit 16, 17

- Week 7: Fast review of algebraic properties and simplifying expressions; literal
  equations (rearranging formulas for a specific variable) and absolute value operations.
- Week 8: Solving and graphing compound inequalities and absolute value equations.
- Week 9: Introduction to functions: domain, range, mapping diagrams, and function
  notation $f(x)$.
- Week 10: Arithmetic sequences and finding slope/rate of change across different
  representations.
- Weeks 11–12: Graphing and writing linear equations: Slope-Intercept ($y = mx + b$),
  Point-Slope ($y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)$), and Standard Form ($Ax + By = C$).
- Week 13: Graphing linear inequalities in two variables.

**Essential Question:** How do algebraic properties and function notation work together to
model predictable, constant change?

## Unit 3: Quadratic Functions & Equations (Weeks 14–19)

**Core Focus:** Analyzing parabolas and mastering the multiple ways to solve a quadratic
equation.

- Weeks 14–15: Graphing quadratic functions ($f(x) = ax^2 + bx + c$), identifying
  vertices, axes of symmetry, and intercepts.
- Week 16: Solving quadratics by graphing and finding square roots.
- Week 17: Solving quadratics by completing the square.
- Week 18: The Quadratic Formula
  ($x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$) and using the discriminant to check for real
  solutions.
- Week 19: Comparing linear and quadratic models; review.

**Essential Question:** How do the different methods of solving a quadratic equation
expose different properties of a parabola?

## Unit 4: Polynomials & Factoring (Weeks 20–24)

**Core Focus:** Breaking down complex expressions into their structural components.

- Weeks 20–21: Polynomial operations: adding, subtracting, and multiplying (FOIL, area
  models).
- Week 22: Factoring out the Greatest Common Factor (GCF) and factoring by grouping.
- Weeks 23–24: Factoring quadratic trinomials ($x^2 + bx + c$ and $ax^2 + bx + c$) and
  special products (difference of squares).

**Essential Question:** How is factoring a polynomial similar to finding the prime
factorization of an integer?

## Unit 5: Exponents, Radicals, & Exponential Functions (Weeks 25–30)

**Core Focus:** Shifting from constant addition (linear) to constant multiplication
(exponential).
**AOPS Algebra** Unit 19, 21

- Weeks 25–26: Rational exponents, radical properties, and simplifying radical
  expressions.
- Weeks 27–28: Geometric sequences and graphing exponential functions ($f(x) = ab^x$).
- Weeks 29–30: Modeling exponential growth and decay (e.g., compound interest, population
  dynamics); comparing linear, exponential, and quadratic models; final course review.

**Essential Question:** What makes exponential growth fundamentally different from linear
growth over time?
