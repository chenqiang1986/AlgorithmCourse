# Course 3 Syllabus

A 36-week, 8th-grade pre-algebra syllabus covering the six units below, each anchored by
an essential question that frames why the unit's skills matter.

## Unit 1: The Number System & Exponents (Weeks 1–6)

**Core Focus:** Transitioning from concrete numbers to abstract algebraic notation.

- Weeks 1–2: Rational vs. irrational numbers; plotting irrationals on a number line.
- Weeks 3–4: Laws of integer exponents (multiplication, division, power of a power).
- Weeks 5–6: Scientific notation (operations and real-world conversions).

**Essential Question:** How do we represent and operate on extremely large or extremely
small quantities in the real world?

## Unit 2: Expressions & Linear Equations (Weeks 7–14)

**Core Focus:** The backbone of 8th-grade math — moving from multi-step equations to
systems.

- Weeks 7–8: Simplifying expressions using the distributive property and combining like
  terms.
- Weeks 9–10: Solving linear equations with variables on both sides (identifying 1, 0, or
  infinite solutions).
- Weeks 11–12: Introduction to systems of linear equations (solving by graphing).
- Weeks 13–14: Solving systems algebraically (substitution and basic elimination).

**Essential Question:** What does it mean for two different situations to have the exact
same value or outcome?

## Unit 3: Functions & Linear Relationships (Weeks 15–22)

**Core Focus:** Defining mathematical relationships and analyzing change.

- Weeks 15–16: Defining a function (inputs, outputs, vertical line test).
- Weeks 17–18: Rate of change and slope ($m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$) from graphs,
  tables, and coordinates.
- Weeks 19–20: Graphing linear functions using slope-intercept form ($y = mx + b$).
- Weeks 21–22: Comparing different functions represented in different ways (e.g., a graph
  vs. an equation).

**Essential Question:** How can we use mathematical models to predict future trends?

## Unit 4: Transformational Geometry (Weeks 23–27)

**Core Focus:** Moving shapes on a coordinate plane to understand congruence and
similarity.

- Weeks 23–24: Rigid transformations (translations, reflections, rotations) and
  congruence.
- Weeks 25–26: Non-rigid transformations (dilations) and scale factor.
- Week 27: Angle relationships (interior/exterior angles of triangles, parallel lines cut
  by a transversal).

**Essential Question:** How do geometric shapes maintain or change their properties when
moved through space?

## Unit 5: Real-World Geometry & Right Triangles (Weeks 28–32)

**Core Focus:** Applying the Pythagorean theorem and finding 3D volumes.

- Weeks 28–29: The Pythagorean theorem ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$) and finding distance on a
  coordinate plane.
- Weeks 30–32: Volume formulas for cylinders, cones, and spheres.

**Essential Question:** How does geometry help us measure and optimize physical space?

## Unit 6: Bivariate Data & Statistics (Weeks 33–36)

**Core Focus:** Looking for patterns in data sets with two variables.

- Weeks 33–34: Constructing and interpreting scatter plots (positive, negative, or no
  association).
- Week 35: Drawing lines of best fit and writing informal linear equations for data.
- Week 36: Two-way relative frequency tables and final course review.

**Essential Question:** What patterns and relationships can we find in real-world data?
