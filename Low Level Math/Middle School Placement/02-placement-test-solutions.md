## Full Solutions

### Problem 1: Multi-Digit Multiplication

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Break 26 into $20 + 6$ and multiply each part, then add:

$$347 \times 26 = 347 \times 20 + 347 \times 6 = 6940 + 2082 = 9022$$

The answer is **(A) 9022**.

</details>

### Problem 2: Long Division

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

$24 \times 200 = 4800$, which leaves a remainder of $4896 - 4800 = 96$. Since
$24 \times 4 = 96$:

$$4896 \div 24 = 200 + 4 = 204$$

The answer is **(B) 204**.

</details>

### Problem 3: Adding and Subtracting Decimals

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Work left to right, keeping the decimal points aligned:

$$12.6 + 8.75 - 3.4 = 21.35 - 3.4 = 17.95$$

The answer is **(C) 17.95**.

</details>

### Problem 4: Multiplying Decimals

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply as whole numbers, $32 \times 15 = 480$, then place the decimal point so the
result has as many decimal places as both factors combined (one $+$ one $= 2$):

$$3.2 \times 1.5 = 4.80 = 4.8$$

The answer is **(D) 4.8**.

</details>

### Problem 5: Dividing Decimals

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply both numbers by 10 so the divisor is a whole number, then divide:

$$7.2 \div 0.6 = 72 \div 6 = 12$$

The answer is **(E) 12**.

</details>

### Problem 6: Adding Fractions With Unlike Denominators

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Rewrite $\dfrac{2}{3}$ with a denominator of 6, then add:

$$\frac{2}{3} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{4}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{5}{6}$$

The answer is **(A) $\dfrac{5}{6}$**.

</details>

### Problem 7: Subtracting Fractions With Unlike Denominators

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Rewrite $\dfrac{1}{4}$ with a denominator of 8, then subtract:

$$\frac{7}{8} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{7}{8} - \frac{2}{8} = \frac{5}{8}$$

The answer is **(B) $\dfrac{5}{8}$**.

</details>

### Problem 8: Multiplying Fractions

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiply numerators together and denominators together (no common denominator needed):

$$\frac{3}{5} \times \frac{2}{7} = \frac{3 \times 2}{5 \times 7} = \frac{6}{35}$$

The answer is **(C) $\dfrac{6}{35}$**.

</details>

### Problem 9: Dividing a Fraction by a Whole Number

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Dividing by 3 is the same as multiplying by $\dfrac{1}{3}$:

$$\frac{3}{4} \div 3 = \frac{3}{4} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3}{12} = \frac{1}{4}$$

The answer is **(D) $\dfrac{1}{4}$**.

</details>

### Problem 10: Comparing Fractions and Decimals

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Convert everything to decimals to compare directly:

$$0.6 = 0.600, \quad 0.58, \quad \frac{3}{5} = 0.600, \quad \frac{5}{8} = 0.625$$

The greatest value is $0.625 = \dfrac{5}{8}$.

The answer is **(E) $\dfrac{5}{8}$**.

</details>

### Problem 11: Place Value / Powers of Ten

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Multiplying by 100 shifts the decimal point two places to the right:

$$4.7 \times 100 = 470$$

The answer is **(A) 470**.

</details>

### Problem 12: Order of Operations

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Parentheses first, then multiplication, then addition:

$$5 + 2 \times (9 - 4) = 5 + 2 \times 5 = 5 + 10 = 15$$

The answer is **(B) 15**.

</details>

### Problem 13: Area

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Find the area of the whole garden, then subtract the area taken up by the patio:

$$12 \times 7 - 4 \times 4 = 84 - 16 = 68 \text{ ft}^2$$

The answer is **(C) 68 ft²**.

</details>

### Problem 14: Volume of a Rectangular Prism

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Volume is length times width times height:

$$V = 6 \times 4 \times 3 = 72 \text{ units}^3$$

The answer is **(D) 72 units³**.

</details>

### Problem 15: Perimeter Word Problem

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

The length is twice the width, so length $= 2 \times 15 = 30$ m. Perimeter is twice the sum
of length and width:

$$P = 2 \times (30 + 15) = 2 \times 45 = 90 \text{ m}$$

The answer is **(E) 90 m**.

</details>

### Problem 16: Coordinate Plane

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Moving right increases the $x$-coordinate; moving down decreases the $y$-coordinate:

$$(3 + 4, \; 5 - 2) = (7, 3)$$

The answer is **(A) $(7, 3)$**.

</details>

### Problem 17: Line Plot / Mean

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Add all the values and divide by how many there are:

$$\frac{3 + 3.5 + 4 + 3.5 + 4.5}{5} = \frac{18.5}{5} = 3.7 \text{ in}$$

The answer is **(B) 3.7 in**.

</details>

### Problem 18: Multi-Step Word Problem

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Find the total cost, then subtract from \$20:

$$4 \times 3.25 = 13.00, \qquad 13.00 + 2.50 = 15.50, \qquad 20.00 - 15.50 = 4.50$$

The answer is **(C) \$4.50**.

</details>

### Problem 19: Numerical Patterns

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

List a few terms of each pattern:

$$\text{A: } 0, 3, 6, 9, 12, \ldots \qquad \text{B: } 0, 6, 12, 18, 24, \ldots$$

Each term of B is exactly double the corresponding term of A (since B adds 6 each step
while A adds 3 — twice as much per step).

The answer is **(D) Each term of B is twice the corresponding term of A**.

</details>

### Problem 20: Challenge — Unit Rate Reasoning

<details>
<summary>Solution</summary>

Find the price of one notebook, then scale up to 10:

$$7.50 \div 3 = 2.50 \text{ per notebook}, \qquad 2.50 \times 10 = 25.00$$

The answer is **(E) \$25.00**.

</details>

## Interpreting the Results and Track Recommendation

This test is scored out of 20. Problems 1-18 are straight 5th-grade grade-level skills;
problems 19-20 run slightly ahead of grade level and are the strongest signal for
acceleration readiness.

- **15-20 correct: Faster track.** The student has both fluency in grade-level skills and
  early success reasoning about patterns and rates. Recommend starting middle school math
  on the accelerated pathway with [Course 3](../Course%203). Use the
  [topic map](./01-placement-test.md#topic-map) to patch any single missed topic before
  starting.
- **11-14 correct: Borderline.** Grade-level fluency is present but inconsistent, or
  problems 19-20 were missed. A teacher or parent should weigh in — a student who missed
  only computational slips (problems 1-5) but solved 19-20 correctly is a reasonable
  faster-track candidate; a student who struggled broadly across fractions and decimals
  (problems 3, 4, 6-9) is better served by the slower track.
- **Below 11 correct: Slower track.** Recommend the standard-paced 6th grade math course.
  Use the [topic map](./01-placement-test.md#topic-map) to target review over the summer,
  focusing first on whole-number operations, decimals, and fractions (problems 1-9), since
  every later topic builds on them.
